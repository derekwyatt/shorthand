# Manual Unit 35: name terminations; cities and states joined; "State of" (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter XII, Unit 35, printed
pages 151–154 (PDF pages 167–170 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit34.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` / `i`
circles, the `o` / `u` hooks, the `h` dot, written in writing order; vowel marks are
omitted. `_` starts a disjoined ending written as its own stroke just under the end of the
word (as in Unit 32). Plate crop boxes (80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in
`scratchpad/u35/plates-unit35.json`, ids `word-<slug>` for the Words table and
`phrase-<slug>` for the Phrases table. Page 154 (§245) is reading and dictation practice.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§241 Name terminations.** "The terminations burg, ville, field, port may generally be
  expressed by the first letter, joined or disjoined as convenient; ford, by fd; ington, by
  a disjoined tn; and ingham, by a disjoined m:" The words: Harrisburg, Petersburg,
  Fitchburg, Newburgh, Danville, Zanesville, Evansville, Knoxville, Pittsfield, Plainfield,
  Davenport, Newport, Shreveport, Oxford, Rockford, Milford, Kensington, Arlington,
  Birmingham, Nottingham. A note: "A distinction between ton and town is made as follows:"
  Johnston, Johnstown, Charleston, Charlestown.
- **§242 Cities and states joined.** "The names of cities and states often may be joined:"
  Buffalo, N. Y.; St. Paul, Minn.; Boston, Mass.; Detroit, Mich.; Chicago, Ill.; Denver,
  Colo.; Omaha, Nebr.; St. Louis, Mo.; Rochester, N. Y.; Baltimore, Md.; Memphis, Tenn.;
  Louisville, Ky.; Minneapolis, Minn.; Washington, D. C.
- **§243 "State of" joined.** "When the words "State of" precede the name of a state, omit
  of and join the words, if convenient:" State of N. Y., Nebr., Ill., N. J., Miss., Mass.,
  Pa., La., Ga., Minn.
- **§244 Canadian provinces and cities** (including Newfoundland and Labrador): Prince
  Edward Island, Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Quebec, Ontario, Manitoba, Saskatchewan,
  Alberta, British Columbia, Yukon, N. W. Territories, Labrador, Newfoundland, Brantford,
  Calgary, Edmonton, Hamilton, London, Montreal, Ottawa, Peterboro, Regina, St. John,
  Saskatoon, Toronto, Vancouver, Victoria, Windsor, Winnipeg, Saint John's.
- **§245 Reading and dictation practice**: connected text, not transcribed.

Newport and Birmingham have their ending disjoined: the p of port starts just left of the
end of new, under it; the m of ingham lies under the circle at the foot of the b, a little
right. Rockford and Milford join ford as fd.

## Left out, and why

- **§241, the left column (Harrisburg, Petersburg, Fitchburg, Newburgh, Danville,
  Zanesville, Evansville, Knoxville, Pittsfield, Plainfield):** the outlines are packed
  into one column and run into each other on the plate (each disjoined b or v sits under
  its neighbour), so the bodies can't be read without guessing.
- **§241, Davenport, Shreveport, Oxford:** Davenport's body can't be split into tokens;
  Shreveport's p and Oxford overlap on the plate.
- **§241 note (Johnston, Johnstown, Charleston, Charlestown):** the ton/town distinction is
  a small difference at the end of the outline that can't be read with certainty at the
  plate's size.
- **§242, every pair:** the joined city-and-state outlines are long and run several
  syllables together; they can't be split into tokens without guessing.
- **§243, State of N. J.:** as for N. J. in Unit 34, the n is written through the j.
- **§243, State of N. Y., Nebr., Miss., Mass.:** the state's part after the t can't be
  read with certainty (a curl, a loop or a hook of uncertain value).
- **§243, State of Ill.:** `s-right t e l` is the same outline as word-steel.
- **§244, every name:** long multi-syllable outlines too small to split without guessing
  (Hamilton also needs the h dot).
- **§245:** reading practice only.

## Inference

- ford as `f d` follows the rule's "ford, by fd"; on the plates the f runs down-left into
  a long rising d with a sharp angle.
- The State-of phrases use brief-state (`s-right t`) as written elsewhere in the app; the
  plate shows the small s and rising t at the start of each.

## Engine needs

- A placement for a disjoined ending written *after* the end of the word, on or just
  below the line (the ington and Nottingham signs; see the needing-new-marks table). The
  existing `_` places the ending under the end, which fits Newport and Birmingham only.

# Manual Unit 32: analogical word-endings, disjoined (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter XI, Unit 32, printed
pages 137–140 (PDF pages 153–156 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit31.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` / `i`
circles, the `o` / `u` hooks, the `h` dot and the `w-dash`, written in writing order; vowel
marks are omitted. `_` starts a disjoined suffix: the tokens after it are written as a
separate stroke under the end of the word. Plate crop boxes (80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in
`scratchpad/u32/plates-unit32.json`, ids `word-<word>`; they cover the Words table only.
Pages 139 (from §232) and 140 are reading and dictation practice.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§230 Analogical word-endings, disjoined.** "In most of the disjoined word-endings the
  vowel preceding the ending is understood, as in art(i)cle, barn(a)cle, dom(e)stic,
  cal(a)mity, extr(e)mity, auth(o)rity, sec(u)rity:" The rows are -ical, -icle, -acle;
  -tic, -tical, -tically; -lity; -ulate, -ulation; -bility; -city; -logy, -logical; -rity;
  -fication; -ograph-y; -egraph-y; -gram, -grim; -ward, -hood; -ship; -mental; -mity,
  -nity; -stic. The footnote to -ward: "In the words forward, afterward, upward, backward
  the suffix is joined; in other words, it is disjoined."
- **§231 Key to analogical word-endings.**
  1. article, practical, physical, musical, medical, technical.
  2. politic, political, politically, critic, critical, critically.
  3. personality, locality, formality, facility, utility.
  4. formulate, formulation, speculate, speculation, regulate, regulation.
  5. possibility, ability, sensibility, nobility, reliability, adaptability, visibility.
  6. capacity, simplicity, ferocity, tenacity, scarcity, sagacity, electricity.
  7. psychology, apology, analogy, physiology, theology, zoölogy, genealogy, mineralogy.
  8. authority, majority, prosperity, security, sincerity, popularity, minority.
  9. classification, specification, modification, notification, qualification,
     justification.
  10. phonograph, photography, geography, lithography, stenography, typography,
      typographic.
  11. telegraph, telegraphy, telegrapher, telegraphic, calligraphy.
  12. telegram, cablegram, radiogram, pilgrim, program.
  13. forward, afterward, upward, backward, reward, boyhood, neighborhood, childhood.

## Words needing new marks

The engine now has `_` for the common placement (the suffix under the end of the word).
The words below still need either another placement or a reading of their bodies.

Placement from the plates (p. 137–138, read at 300 dpi). The suffix is a stroke of its
own. Horizontally it starts at, or a little left of, the x of the word's last point
(about 0–0.3 of a line left of it); for long suffix signs that run rightwards (the l of
-lity, the r of -rity, the g of -gram) it runs on past the end of the word. Vertically its
top sits just below the word's last point, about 0.2–0.4 of a line lower, close but not
touching. Exceptions seen on the plates:

- **-bility** (b): the b is a long downward curve written *below the start* of the word,
  not its end (possibility, ability, sensibility, …): the b's top is under the word's
  first stroke.
- **-ulate / -ulation** (the oo hook, with sh for -ulation): a small hook just right of and
  below the word's end, at about the baseline of the word's last stroke.
- **-ward, -hood** disjoined (d): a full upward d written just to the right of the word's
  end, parallel to it, its foot level with the word's lowest point (reward, boyhood,
  neighborhood, childhood) — a placement like the §223 `|` in `manual-unit30.md`.
- **-egraph-y**, **-ograph-y** (a small circle and f, or the o hook and f): written just
  above-right of the word's end, at about the height of its top.

## Left out, and why

- **classification:** its crop is tangled with a neighbour and the body is an uncertain reading.
- **-bility (7):** the b is written under the *start* of the word, not its end.
- **-ograph-y, -egraph-y (12):** the suffix is written above-right of the word.
- **-ulate, -ulation (6):** the hook sits beside the end of the word, at its baseline, not
  under it.
- **-ward, -hood disjoined (4):** reward, boyhood, neighborhood, childhood: the d stands
  beside the end of the word (the `|` placement of `manual-unit30.md`).
- **-tic, -tical, -tically (6):** the -tic sign is a lone circle.
- **-mity, -nity (4):** on the plate the ending looks joined to the word (no pen lift can be
  seen), so the reading of a disjoined sign isn't certain.
- **courtship, hardship, partnership, utility:** the suffix is beside the end, not under it.
- **personality, majority, simplicity:** the body can't be read without guessing (the hook
  after p r s in personality; whether majority's r is joined to the foot of the j; the
  loop at the start of simplicity).
- **The rest of the -ical, -lity, -city, -logy, -rity, -fication, -gram, -ship, -mental
  and -stic rows:** the bodies can't be read without guessing (article and medical also
  show no separate k).
- **upward** `p d`: the same outline as paid (Unit 25, `word-paid`).
- **backward:** the b, the loop at its foot and the k before the d can't be split into
  tokens without guessing.
- **§232:** connected reading practice only.

## Inference

- forward as `f d` and afterward as `a f d`: the -ward sign is read as the upward d, which
  the plates show as a long upstroke from the foot of the f. The t of after is not
  written.

## Engine needs

- Further suffix placements: -bility under the start of the word; -ograph/-egraph
  above-right; the hook of -ulate and the d of -ward/-hood beside the end.
- `_` placement against the plates (contact sheet): security's r sits about 0.5–1 U
  further right than the engine draws it and only about 0.3 U below the word's last
  point, not 0.75 U; locality, formality, popularity, classification and township are
  close.
- A lone circle as a disjoined suffix (-tic).

# Manual Unit 24: phrasing principles, words omitted (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter VIII, Unit 24, printed
pages 104–107 (PDF pages 120–123 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit2.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` / `i`
circles, the `o` / `u` hooks and the `h` dot, written in writing order; vowel marks are
omitted. Plate crop boxes (80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in
`scratchpad/u24/plates-unit24.json`, ids `phrase-<slug of the whole title>`. Pages 106–107
(§190–191) are reading and writing practice.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§189 Words Omitted.** "Any unimportant word may be omitted where the sense requires its
  restoration in transcribing:"
  Notes: "1. To secure facility in execution, split up long phrases and practice
  progressively, as, for example, I should, I should like, I should like to know."
  "2. The use of such expressions as in reply to your, for the time being, in regard to the
  matter, etc. is to be discouraged. They are not sanctioned by careful writers of English.
  Nevertheless they are still widely in use in business correspondence, and to prepare
  students for the kind of dictation they will receive, it is necessary to draw attention
  to these phrases."
- **§190 Reading and dictation practice**: connected text, not transcribed.
- **§191 Writing practice**: sentences only, not transcribed.

## Left out, and why

- **Joinings too small or tangled to read with confidence** (several brief forms and
  omitted words run together in one outline, and the readings would be guesses): in the
  world, ought to be, ought to have, ought to receive, day or two, here and there, in reply
  to your, for the time being, out of the question, one of the most, son-in-law, one of
  our, sooner or later, in order to see, some of those, in reference to the matter, in
  regard to the matter (p. 104); up to the time, by the way, on the market, on the subject,
  in the market, hand in hand, that is to say, able to say, glad to see, kindly let us
  know, in order to prepare, little or nothing, one of the best, in a day or two, on
  account of the way, I should like to have, I should like to know (p. 105).
- **Readings that do not match the plate**: in such a manner (the circle after the men blend
  doesn't come out as on the plate) and question of time (an uncertain reading of the plate).
- **§190–191**: reading and writing practice.

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- The readings use the brief forms of earlier units: more `m`, little `l e t`, week `e k`,
  some `s-right m`, question `k sh`, time `tem`, matter `m a t`, and `nd`, I `i`, am `m`,
  opinion `o p n`, such `s-right ch`, manner `men e-rev`, the hooks for one (`u n`) and of
  the o hook in on (`o n`).
- two is read as t with the oo hook (`t u`), the outline rising from the end of the phrase;
  the plates show a short upward stroke ending in a small hook.
- them is read `th-over m`: after some the plate rises in a curve and runs on level.
- no is `n o`, less `l e s-left` (as in Unit 5), with the left s after the circle on l.

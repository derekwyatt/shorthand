# Manual Unit 11: method of expressing w (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter IV, Unit 11, printed
pages 52–55 (PDF pages 68–71 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025. Page 55 (§103) is
connected reading practice, not transcribed.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit10.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` circles,
the §71 `a-rev` / `e-rev`, and the `h` dot; vowel marks are omitted. `u` is the oo hook,
which by §97 also expresses w. Plate crop boxes (80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in
`scratchpad/u11/plates-unit11.json`, ids `word-<word>`, `brief-<first word>`,
`phrase-<words>`. Plates were read at 300–2100 px per inch crops.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§97.** "By pronouncing the following words slowly it will be found that w has the sound
  of oo; therefore w is expressed by the oo-hook:" we = oo-ē, wave = oo-ā-v, wall = oo-aw-l.
- **§98 Word drill.** Footnote: "In the combination wh, as in wheel, the h is sounded first."
- **§99 W Within Words.** "In the body of a word it is more convenient to express w by a
  dash placed beneath the vowel following. In writing sw and a circle vowel, as in sweet,
  swim, swell, the hook for w is preferable to the dash:"
- **§100 A Before W or H.** "In words beginning with a-h or a-w the dot, placed on the line
  close to the next character, is used to express a:" ahead, awake, await, away, awoke,
  awaken.
- **§101 Frequent phrases.**
- **§102 Brief forms for common words.**
- **§103 Reading and dictation practice** (p. 55): connected text only.

## Left out, and why

- **wool** (`w oo l`, p. 53): two oo hooks in a row (w and oo); the plate shows one
  compressed curl and the joining of two hooks is not defined.
- **water** (`w aw ter`, p. 53): the hook, o hook and the ter ending are too compressed to
  read.
- **whirl** (`hw ĕ r l`, p. 53): r runs into l as one long curve; that joining is not
  established in earlier units.
- **Brief forms world, house / whose, suppose, reply, whether, follow / fall, further,
  body, accept / -ance, explain, duty, particular, bring, nature, report (§102):** their
  endings, s signs, loops or the ing dot cannot be read with confidence at print size, or
  they need diphthongs (house, Unit 13) or marks the engine lacks (bring: the ing dot).
- **§103:** connected reading text.

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- **Circle size** follows the vowel group; the plates agree (weighed, wait, wane, wake,
  wear, whale: large; wet, wed, win, wicked, weep, whim: small).
- **s signs.** waste, wages and the sw words take the right s: §49 names no rule for s
  next to the oo hook or after j, so "all other cases" applies; the plates agree where
  legible (waste's s crosses the t and is the least clear).
- **width** ends in the right-motion `th-over` by §78 (th not next to o, r, l); the plate
  shows the th curving over.
- **wall** is `u o l` (oo-aw-l): the oo hook then the o hook.
- **we** in the §101 phrases is `u e`, as in §97; the plates show the hook and small circle
  joined to the following word's stroke.
- **ahead** is written with the a dot followed by the e circle and d; the h (normally a dot)
  is not written separately on the plate.

## Engine needs

1. **Initial w = oo hook before a circle** (we, way, wet, wait, win … every §98 word): the
   hook is followed directly by an a or e circle, then the next stroke. On the plates the
   hook flows into the circle with no angle and the circle sits inside the hook's turn
   (way, wet, wake on p. 52). The engine must join a circle after `u` (u is the "stroke"
   on one side; the circle's rotation should continue the hook's clockwise turn).
2. **h dot before the oo hook** (wheat, wheel, whim, whip, whale, p. 53): the dot sits above
   the hook, as for the o hook (home); the engine supports h before `o` only.
3. **s before the oo hook** (sweet, swim, swell, swift, p. 53): a right s runs into the hook.
4. **§99 w-dash:** a short horizontal dash written *beneath* the vowel circle (or o hook)
   that follows the w, disjoined, after the outline is written: quick (dash under the
   e circle between k and k), queen, quit, quote (under the o hook), acquit, equity,
   queer, twin, dwell, squall, doorway (p. 53). It is about the width of the circle,
   level, just below it. Proposed token `w-dash` before the circle it marks.
5. **§100 a-dot:** a dot on the line, close before the first stroke (ahead, awake, await,
   away, awoke, awaken, p. 54); unlike the h dot it is not above a circle. Proposed token
   `a-dot` at the start of the outline.

# Manual Unit 9: the th joinings, con / com, ly, phrasing principles, brief forms (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter III, Unit 9, printed
pages 42–47 (PDF pages 58–63 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit2.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` circles and
the `h` dot, written in writing order; vowel marks are omitted. `o` is the o hook (Unit 7).
The **left-motion th** is `th-under` (bulges down to the right, counter-clockwise); the
**right-motion th** is `th-over` (bulges up to the left, clockwise), as in Unit 1's lesson.
Plate crop boxes (80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in `scratchpad/u9/plates-unit9.json`.
Pages 46–47 (§89 reading, §90 writing practice) are connected text only.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§78.** "The left-motion th is used before and after o, r, l. In other cases the
  right-motion th is used." Footnote: "The word although is a combination of all and
  though."
- **§79.** "When th is the only consonant stroke, as in the brief forms for that or they,
  or is in combination with s, the right-motion th is used, as in these and seethe."
  (Text only; no plates.)
- **§80 Frequent prefixes and suffixes.** "The prefixes con, com, coun, cog, followed by a
  consonant, are expressed by k. The suffix ly is expressed by a small circle; ily and
  ally, by a loop."
- **§81.** "In words beginning with comm or conn, the second m or n is written" …
  "When con or com is followed by a vowel or by r or l, write kn for con and km for com."
- **§82.** "After a circle vowel, ly is written on the opposite side from the vowel."
- **§83.** "To express the plural of some brief forms ending in a circle and of some words
  ending in a loop, a slight change is made in the manner of joining s."
- **§84 Phrasing principles.** "Before words beginning with a downward character or o, r,
  l, the word to is expressed by t."
- **§85.** "When repeated in a phrase, as is expressed by s."
- **§86.** "After be or been, the word able is expressed by a."
- **§87 Frequent phrases.**
- **§88 Brief forms for common words.** Footnotes: "The prefix form for agr-e-i, a loop
  written above the following character, is used to express the word agree." "The angle
  between k and p is maintained in the word complete to make a distinction between
  complete and keep."

The s signs in the phrases follow §49 and agree with the plates where they can be read:
after t a left s (to see, to say, of its); before and after l a left s (as well as,
as low as); before m, g and after ch, g a right s (as much as, as good as, which is);
before n a right s (is not). The as … as plates show the first s as a small curl
before the stroke. `the` is th-over and `their` th-under, as in the level 2 brief forms;
of their follows §78 (th after o is the left-motion form).

## Left out, and why

- **theater (§78):** the joining after the circle (t, r and a loop at the top) cannot be
  read reliably.
- **council, compel (§80):** council's s and circle are tangled at the top; compel shows
  no circle between p and l, so the vowel cannot be placed.
- **convention, convince, concrete (§80):** the endings (n-shun, n-s, k-r-e-t) are too
  small to read stroke by stroke.
- **hardly (§80):** the plate shows h, the a circle, d and the ly circle with no visible
  r; the manual does not say why, so it is left out rather than guessed.
- **ily / ally loop words (§80): formally, easily, hastily, readily, family, totally,
  socially, heartily:** the final loop has no token (see Engine needs).
- **commence (§81):** no circle is visible between m and n, and the final s is unclear.
- **§82 dearly, daily, nearly:** ly is a second circle written on the opposite side of the
  vowel circle (see Engine needs).
- **§83 names, letters, families:** a changed s joining on brief-form plurals and loops.
- **Phrases with the u hook** (not verified): to work, with you, you are, you know, on
  your, from you, your letter, you may, if you will, you would.
- **Phrases with that / this / these, or brief forms not yet transcribed:** that is, of
  these, with that, more than, this is, that this, to this, at that, as to, to give,
  to take; to honor (the end of honor is unclear); as great as, as many as (the
  middle of great / many is not clear).
- **in his:** whether the s after n is left or right cannot be read on the plate (§49
  would give a left s).
- **Brief forms:** send (s and the nd blend unclear), represent, agree (a disjoined loop
  above the next character), already, ask (the a circle is not visible), employ,
  official, immediate / immediately, express, knowledge (endings not clear enough);
  committee (`k t e` on the plate, the same outline as county in this unit).

## Changes to other levels

None. Two outlines repeat examples at other levels, as ever / eve did in Unit 4:
he will (`e l`) is word-ill (level 3), and week / weak (`e k`) is word-eke.

## Engine needs

- **The ily / ally loop (§80).** A small loop at the end of the outline, longer and
  narrower than a circle, in formally (`f m` + loop), family (`f m` + loop), easily
  (`e s-right` + loop), hastily, readily, totally, socially, heartily. Proposed token
  `ly-loop`. Plates: p. 43, right column.
- **ly after a circle vowel (§82).** Two circles in a row, the second (ly) on the
  opposite side from the first: daily `d a e`, dearly and nearly (`d e r e`, `n e r e`,
  where r is hard to see on the plate). The engine joins a circle only next to a stroke.
  Proposed: allow `a e` / `e e` with the second circle turned the other way. Plates:
  p. 43, §82.

# Manual Unit 31: analogical word-endings, joined (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter XI, Unit 31, printed
pages 134–136 (PDF pages 150–152 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit30.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` / `i`
circles, the `o` / `u` hooks, the `h` dot and the `w-dash`, written in writing order; vowel
marks are omitted. `^` ends a disjoined prefix: the tokens before it are written just above
the start of the rest. Plate crop boxes (80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in
`scratchpad/u31/plates-unit31.json`, ids `word-<word>`; they cover the Words table only.
Page 136 (§229) is reading and dictation practice.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§227 Analogical word-endings, joined.** The manual gives no rule sentence, only the
  rows: -scribe, -scription; -cient, -tient, -ciency; -pose, -position; -pute, -putation;
  -ure, -ture; -ual, -tual; -spect, -spection; -quire; -pire; -nsive; -gency; -sure,
  -jury, -jure; -flect, -flection, -flict, -fliction; -sult; -nment. Two notes:
  "Occasionally a disjoined prefix precedes a joined suffix, as in circumspect,
  introspect, in which case the disjoined sign is written above." and, for -nment, "The
  "jog" is omitted in the termination -nment."
- **§228 Key to analogical word-endings.**
  1. subscribe, subscription, describe, description, inscribe, prescription.
  2. ancient, patient, impatient, efficient, efficiency, deficient, deficiency.
  3. compose, composition, propose, proposition, suppose, supposition, dispose,
     disposition, oppose, opposition.
  4. repute, reputation, dispute, disputation, compute, computation, impute, amputation.
  5. secure, picture, feature, nature, creature, departure.
  6. actual, mutual, equal, eventual, continual.
  7. inspect, inspection, prospect, prospective, expectation, circumspect, introspection.
  8. require, inquire, acquire, requirement, acquires.
  9. expire, inspire, conspire, transpire, perspire, aspire.
  10. expensive, extensive, comprehensive, offensive, intensive, defensive.
  11. agency, emergency, exigency, urgency, contingency.
  12. assure, leisure, pressure, measure, treasure, injure.
  13. reflect, reflection, inflict, infliction, conflict, confliction.
  14. insult, result, consult, consultation.
  15. assignment, refinement, consignment, adjournment.
- **§229 Reading and dictation practice**: connected text, not transcribed.

## Left out, and why

- **Same outline as a brief form already in the app:** inspect (`n s-left p`, the brief
  form newspaper / inspect), require (`r k i`) and inquire (`n k i`).
- **Plate too tangled or unclear to read without guessing:** impatient (its crop runs into
  propose and proposition below); efficiency and deficiency (the -ciency ending, a small
  loop and a further curl under sh, can't be split into tokens); departure (no a circle or
  t can be told apart after the p); continual (the run of short strokes before l);
  prospective (overlaps dispose's d); acquire and acquires (a long stroke crosses the
  circles, and the second circle of acquires is drawn as a large loop); perspire,
  expensive, extensive, comprehensive and offensive (the -pire and -nsive rows overlap on
  the plate).
- **-scribe, -scription, all six (subscribe, subscription, describe, description, inscribe, prescription):** the ending on the plate is a sharp zigzag that `s-left k r` doesn't draw.
- **-gency, all five (agency, emergency, exigency, urgency, contingency):** the ending is a
  short j with a small loop and a tail at its foot; whether that is `j e`, `j e s` or
  `j n s` can't be read with confidence, and `j e` draws a much longer j than the plate.
- **-sult, all four (insult, result, consult, consultation):** the ending is a short
  downstroke with a turned foot like the -pute ending; it can't be split into s, the oo
  hook, l and t without guessing.
- **§229:** connected reading practice only.

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- **-scribe, -scription:** `s-left k r` (+ `sh`): after the small s the plate's stroke is a
  long reverse curve, twice a k, read as k and r run together. The de of describe has no
  circle on the plate, so it is `d s-left`, not the `d e` of deck.
- **-cient, -tient:** `sh t`: a short downstroke and a short upstroke, the check mark after
  n in ancient and at the foot of p in patient.
- **-pose / -position:** `p s-right` / `p sh` (position is the brief form `p sh`); com is
  `k`, pro `p r`, sup `s-right`, dis `d s-left`, op the o hook.
- **-pute / -putation:** `p t` / `p t sh`; the re of repute is a lone r with no circle on
  the plate.
- **-ure, -ture:** `k r` after k (secure, picture), `t r` after a vowel (feature, nature,
  creature).
- **-ual, -tual:** `l`, with the t of actual and mutual as a short upstroke before it.
- **-spect, -spection:** `s-left p` / `s-left p sh`, as the brief form special / speak. The
  circum and intr signs of circumspect and introspection are written above, as the §227
  note says: circum a small s, intr the n of Unit 28.
- **-quire:** `k i`, as the brief forms require and inquire; requirement adds `m`.
- **-pire:** `s-left p i`, the broken circle at the foot of p.
- **-nsive:** `n s-left v` (intensive takes the ten blend for "inten").
- **-sure, -jure:** `sh u` / `j u`: the plate's ending is a downstroke turned at its foot,
  as the brief form sure (`sh u`); the e circle sits at the top of the sh.
- **-flect, -flict:** `f l k` (+ `sh`), the f and l run into one long z-shaped stroke.
- **-nment:** `n m` with no jog, one long straight line, as the footnote says; assign is
  `a s-right i n`.

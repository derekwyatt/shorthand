# Manual Unit 5: the signs for s (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter II, Unit 5, printed
pages 23–27 (PDF pages 39–43 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit2.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` circles and
the `h` dot; vowel marks (the small ticks and dots under some circles) are omitted.
`s-left` is the left s, "(" written downward with left motion; `s-right` is the right s,
")" written downward with right motion. Plate crop boxes (80 dpi) are in
`scratchpad/u5/plates-unit5.json`, ids `word-<word>` and `brief-<first word>`.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§46.** "The signs for s, written downward, are taken from a small elliptical figure."
  "The right-motion s is called "right s"" / "The left-motion s is called "left s"".
- **§47.** "In practical writing the sound of z is expressed by the sign for s, since no
  confusion arises from using the same character for both sounds in connected writing. We
  already are accustomed to writing and reading s for z in English, as in rays, praise.
  It is seldom necessary to make a distinction between s and z, but when it is, a short
  dash is struck at a right angle to the sign for s to show that it has the sound of z."
  Examples: race, raise, gas, gaze.
- **§48.** "The base of the first consonant of a word rests on the line of writing, but
  when s precedes another consonant, the base of the consonant following the s is placed
  on the line."
- **§49 Initial and Final S.** "1. Before and after p, b, r, and l, and after t, d, n, m,
  and o, the left s is used" … "2. In all other cases the right s is used" … "A circle
  placed outside the angle in any of these joinings does not change the motion."
- **§50 Frequent-word drill.**
- **§51 S Between Strokes.** "When a circle vowel immediately precedes s between strokes,
  treat the s as belonging to the preceding consonant; if the circle follows the s, the s
  should be treated as if it belonged to the following consonant; when s occurs between
  strokes and is not joined to a circle, write the s with the syllable to which it
  belongs."
- **§52 The Ses Sign.** "The ses sound as heard in faces is expressed by joining the two s
  signs as a blend." Note: "In rapid writing, the first s in ses may become obscure, and
  yet the second s, being written contrary to the rule for writing a single s, clearly
  indicates the plural form." Compare face / faces, lease / leases.
- **§53 Brief forms for common words.** Footnote: "The sign for the prefix under written
  above a following character is used for the word under."
- **§54.** "The suffix thing is expressed by a dot in the following words": anything,
  something, everything.
- **§55 Plurals of Brief Forms.** "The plurals of brief forms ending in s are formed by
  adding another s of the same motion" (cause / causes, business / businesses). "In other
  brief forms the plurals are formed by adding s to the singular forms" (parts, changes,
  ships, forms).

**The s-sign rule in short.** Take the consonant the s touches (by §51, the one on the
same side as an adjoining circle; with no circle, the one in its syllable). Left s if
the s comes *before* p, b, r, l, or *after* p, b, r, l, t, d, n, m, o. Right s otherwise:
before k, g, t, d, n, m, f, v, ch, j, sh, th, and after k, g, f, v, ch, j, sh, and when the
s stands with circles only (see, say, as). A circle between the s and the stroke does
not change the choice. In ses the first s follows the rule and the second is the other
one.

## Left out, and why

- **raise, gaze (§47), crazy (§50):** the plates carry the z dash, which has no token.
- **sashes, sketches, sages (§49), ages (§50):** the -es ending is too small on the plate
  to tell whether an e circle is written before the final s.
- **season, affairs, minutes (§50):** the plate junctions (s–e–z–n; f–r with circle;
  men–e–t) are too small to transcribe safely.
- **these (§50):** the th is too small to tell the over form from the under form.
- **gas (§50):** repeats §47 gas.
- **visit, ransack (§51):** the plate shows the s and circle tangled; the order of
  circle and s cannot be read reliably.
- **ceases, thesis, analysis (§52):** the blend shapes are too compressed to separate the
  circle from the two s signs.
- **Brief forms under, first, thorough / three, think / thing, system / says,
  public / publish (§53):** under is a hook written above the next character; think /
  thing need the ing dot; first, three, system and public have joinings (hook, loop,
  reverse curve) that the plate does not make clear enough.
- **§54 anything, something, everything:** the thing dot.
- **§55 plurals (causes, businesses, parts, changes, ships, forms):** a double s of the
  same motion is not a blend the engine draws, and the small plates were not checked
  sign by sign.

## Changes to other levels

- None needed. The only earlier uses of an s sign are the letters themselves and the
  brief form is / his (`s-right`, `app/data/examples.js`). That brief form agrees with
  §49 rule 2 (s with circles only takes the right s).

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- The s sign in each table row is what the plate shows, and in every row checked it
  agrees with §49/§51. The plates were read at 300–600 dpi.
- In ses (§52), the second s is taken to be the opposite of the first. This comes from
  the §52 note ("written contrary to the rule").
- §48 (the consonant after an initial s rests on the line) is a placement rule. The
  layout rests each outline's lowest point on the line, so words like stage and spread
  sit higher than the plate shows.
- The brief forms work (`u k`) and always (`o l s-left`) use the hook tokens. The plates
  show a small hook joined to the next stroke.

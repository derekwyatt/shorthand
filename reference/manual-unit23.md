# Manual Unit 23: omission of d, and the prefixes and suffixes ul, al, sub, less (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter VIII, Unit 23, printed
pages 100–103 (PDF pages 116–119 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit2.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` / `i`
circles, the `o` / `u` hooks and the `h` dot, written in writing order; vowel marks are
omitted. The w of we is the oo hook running into a circle (`u e`, the Unit 11 w loop).
Plate crop boxes (80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in `scratchpad/u23/plates-unit23.json`,
ids `word-<word>` and `phrase-<words>`. Page 102 (§186–187) is a brief-form derivative
drill with a key; page 103 (§188) is reading and dictation practice.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§178.** "When slightly enunciated, d is often omitted:"
- **§179.** "The d is written in the following words:" commend, contend, attend.
- **§180.** "D is omitted when it immediately precedes m or v:"
- **§181.** "In the words admire, advice, advise, advance, coming under this rule, the
  initial vowel also is omitted to facilitate phrasing, as illustrated in the following
  useful phrases:" we admire, in advance, we advise.
- **§182.** "Where the last letter of a primitive form is omitted, the past tense is
  indicated by a disjoined t, thus:" contested, insisted, extended, requested, demanded,
  compounded.
- **§183 Prefixes and suffixes.** "The syllable ul is expressed by the oo-hook; al
  (pronounced aw-l), by the o-hook. The sign al has already been given in the words also,
  almost. Sub is expressed by a joined s; less, by l:" Footnote to alternate: "For
  convenience, the root form of the word alter is retained in derivative forms, although
  the pronunciation changes."
- **§184.** "Before r, l, ch, j, or a hook, s is written contrary to rule to express sub,
  as in suburb, sublime, subchief, subjoin."
- **§185.** "When sub is followed by a circle vowel, s is disjoined and placed on the line
  close to the following character, thus:" subeditor, subhead.
- **§186 Brief-form derivative drill**, with **§187 Key** (not transcribed; see Left out).
- **§188 Reading and dictation practice**: connected text, not transcribed.

## Left out, and why

- **Same outline as an existing example:** mind (`m i n`, word-mine), beyond (`b e n`,
  word-bin; the plate's circle may be the broken circle, which can't be told at plate size),
  extend (`e s-right ten`, word-eastern), pound (`p n`, brief-upon), admire (`m i`,
  brief-mile).
- **Unclear on the plate:** dividend (the def blend and what follows can't be separated),
  admittance (the -tance ending), advocate, admiration, admirable, adventure, adverse,
  adversary, adverb (the stroke after the a-v "9" and whether r is written), advent (its
  outline sits between admirable's and adventure's in the column and can't be assigned
  with confidence), we advise (the v and broken circle after the w loop run upward into a
  curl that can't be read), ulster, ultimatum, alternative, alternate, substance, subside.
- **Plate unreadable or unplaceable:** abound (its plate interlocks with compounds and
  can't be read cleanly), expound (the plate doesn't read clearly against the transcribed
  outline).
- **Words needing new marks:** contested, insisted, extended, requested, demanded,
  compounded, subeditor, subhead (the §182 disjoined t and §185 disjoined s are separate
  strokes the engine doesn't place yet).
- **§184** (suburb, sublime, subchief, subjoin): named in the text only; no outlines printed.
- **§186–187:** the brief-form derivative drill is printed as unlabelled numbered rows with
  a separate key; the outlines can't be matched to their words reliably.
- **§188:** connected reading practice only.

## Changes to other levels

- None. The duplicates above are left out rather than merged, as the earlier examples carry
  their own meanings (mine, bin, eastern, upon, mile).

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- demand is read with the dem blend (`tem`, §155 in Unit 19): the plate's first stroke is
  the long upward curve, not d then m.
- diamond ends in the men blend (mon, as in §125 moun), with the final d omitted.
- pound / expound / compound drop ow before n by §124 (Unit 15) and d by §178, so pound is
  `p n` (the same outline as the brief form upon) and compound is com (`k`, §56 Unit 9) +
  `p n`.
- commend is com (`k`) + men + a written d, as §179 says the d is written.
- advance and advise drop the initial a by §181; the s signs follow §49/§51 (after n: left
  s; after v, with the circle between: right s).
- submit and subway write sub as the joined right s (§183); subway is s + the w loop.
- almanac: al is the o hook (§183), then men, the a circle and k.
- homeless and needless write less as l (§183); homeless keeps the h dot over the hook.
- In the new-marks table, the outline before `t-apart` is the word's primitive form with
  its last letter omitted (§182), read from the plate; requested and insisted are the least
  certain.

## Engine needs

1. **`t-apart` (§182):** a short t (the upward t stroke, about 1 U) written separately,
   after the end of the outline: on the plates it starts a little to the right of the
   outline's end, level with or slightly below it, with a gap of about half a U (contested,
   extended, demanded, compounded, p. 101). Scored like a separate mark (as the w dash).
2. **`s-apart` (§185):** the right s written separately before the outline, on the line,
   close to (about half a U from) the start of the following circle (subeditor, subhead,
   p. 101).
3. **Check on the comparison sheet:** the w loop before m (`u e m i`, we admire), sub as a
   joined s before the w loop (subway), and the ten blend after n (intend).

**Least certain readings:** thoughtless (`th-over o t l`; the first stroke is a short
upward tick), almanac, diamond, subway, and in the new-marks table requested, insisted and
subeditor.

# Manual Unit 25: the abbreviating principle (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter IX, Unit 25, printed
pages 108–111 (PDF pages 124–127 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit2.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` / `i`
circles, the `o` / `u` hooks and the `h` dot, written in writing order; vowel marks are
omitted. Diphthongs: ū = `e u`, ow = `a u` (Unit 13). Plate crop boxes (80 dpi, printed
page numbers) are in `scratchpad/u25/plates-unit25.json`, ids `word-<word>`. Page 108
(§192) is a connected illustration sentence; page 112 (§197) is reading and dictation
practice.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§192.** "The application of the abbreviating principle discussed in Chapter I,
  paragraph 23, many illustrations of which previously have been given, is more or less
  flexible and depends to a large extent upon the familiarity of the writer with the words
  and subject matter in the dictation." "The abbreviating principle is not employed when
  advantage may be taken of analogical or definite word-building rules, and it should not
  be employed when easily written word forms are possible without it. A good rule to apply
  to any word is: When in doubt, write it out."
- **§193 Short Words.** "In a small but useful group of common words—many illustrations of
  which have been given throughout this manual in "Brief Forms for Common Words"—the form
  stops with a diphthong or a strongly accented vowel:"
- **§194 Long Words.** "An analysis of hundreds of words shows that the abbreviations of
  long words fall into three classes, from which the following rules have been
  established:"
- **§195.** "If there is a longhand abbreviation, it is generally used, if it furnishes a
  distinctive outline, as in the words amount (amt.), April (Apr.), balance (bal.),
  memorandum (memo.):" Footnote: "Memoranda is written mema; street is written st only
  with a street name otherwise str."
- **§196.** "Write through the accented syllable if the outline is distinctive.
  Illustrations: abbrev for abbreviate; lang for language; elab for elaborate, etc.:"
- **§197 Reading and dictation practice**: connected text, not transcribed.

## Words needing new marks

None transcribed. The candidates are listed under "Left out": England, America, U.S. and
U.S.A. carry the double dash under the outline that marks a capital (two short parallel
dashes written below and to the right of the outline's end); horse power is the h dot
before p, which the engine accepts only before a vowel circle or hook. A proposed token
for the capital mark would be `cap-dash`, placed like the §99 w dash but doubled and set
below the last stroke's end.

## Left out, and why

- **Same outline as an existing example:** derive (`d r i` = word-dry), grade (`g r a` =
  word-gray), light (`l i` = brief-light), Street (`s-right t` = brief-state), policy
  (`p o l` = word-pole), offer (`o f` = word-off).
- **Capital mark, no token:** England, America, U.S., U.S.A. (double dash under the
  outline).
- **h dot before a stroke:** horse power (h.p.).
- **Unclear on the plate:** boulevard, discount, memorandum, equivalent, free on board,
  etc. (§195); authentic, certificate, cooperate, custom, deposit, illustration /
  illustrate, material (§196). Each runs several signs into a small or tangled form whose
  reading would be a guess.
- **Uncertain reading:** establish (`e s-right t b`), an uncertain reading that doesn't match the plate closely.
- **§192:** the illustration is a connected sentence.
- **§197:** reading and dictation practice.

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- con is `k` (convenience, confuse), pro / pre / pri are `p r` (§127, Unit 15), re is `r`,
  un / in / en are `n` with the initial vowel dropped (§134), as in earlier units.
- ul in ultimo is the oo hook (§183, Unit 23), so ult is `u t`.
- develop uses the def blend (§140, Unit 17), as devel sounds def-v-l.
- The s in decide is the right s, as in decision (Unit 18); in proceed and pleasant the
  left s, by §49 (after r, l).

## Engine needs

None new. To check on the comparison sheet:

- **The ū sign after strokes** (pure, cure, peculiar, confuse, excuse, refuse, union): the
  doubled oo hook after the small circle (Unit 13).
- **`a r i`** (arrive): two circles with r between, the second broken.
- **`p r s-left e`** (proceed): the small s and circle tucked at the foot of r.

## Least certain readings, to check first

peculiar (whether a small circle follows p), refuse (whether e is written after r),
decide (which s), glory (whether the ending is the o hook), pleasant (the s after the
circle), establish (the t), post office, ultimo.

# Manual Unit 16: blended consonants (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter VI, Unit 16, printed
pages 73–76 (PDF pages 89–92 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit2.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` circles, `i`
(the broken circle), the `o` / `u` hooks and the `h` dot, written in writing order; vowel
marks are omitted. The blends of this unit are the existing strokes `nd` (-nt, -nd) and `md`
(-mt, -md). Plate crop boxes (80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in
`scratchpad/u16/plates-unit16.json`, ids `word-<word>` and `brief-<first word>`. Page 76
(§139 reading and dictation practice) is connected plates only.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§133.** "When two straight lines form an obtuse or blunt angle, the natural tendency of
  the hand is to "slur" the angle and allow the lines to form a curve": n + t / d "blended
  becomes" one curve "and expresses -nt, -nd"; m + t / d "blended becomes" one curve "and
  expresses -mt, -md".
- **§134.** "The -nt, -nd blend is an upward curve, corresponding in length to the sign for
  f; the -mt, -md blend is an upward curve, corresponding in length to v. The n or m governs
  the length of the curve; the curve containing m naturally is longer. At the beginning of
  words, short e and short i are omitted before these blends, as in entry, empty, induce,
  etc."
- **§135 Word drill.**
- **§136 The Ld Combination.** "The combination ld is expressed by giving l a swinging
  upward turn at the finish:"
- **§137 Days and months.** Abbreviated forms (not transcribed; see Left out).
- **§138 Brief forms for common words.**
- **§139 Reading and dictation practice**: connected text, not transcribed.

## Left out, and why

- **Same outline as an existing example:** owned (`o nd` = word-want, Unit 7); the brief
  forms copy (`k p` = brief-company / brief-complete), remit / remittance (`r e m` =
  word-rim), invoice (`n v` = brief-never), acknowledge (`a k` = word-ache), oblige (`o b`
  = brief-object; also Unit 14's reading of object), allow (`a l` = word-ail).
- **Too ambiguous on the plate:** apparent (the junction after the initial circle and p
  can't be read), Indian (carries two small marks under the outline, as yet unexplained),
  inventory (the first strokes, n and v, run together); the brief forms entire, receipt,
  stand, attention, industry (the order of circle, s and blend can't be read).
- **§137 days and months:** abbreviated forms; several are small and ambiguous (Monday,
  Tuesday, March, August, October), and May is `m a`, already word-may.
- **§139:** connected reading practice.
- **older, colder:** ld then r: the engine joins them with a peak where the plates run on smoothly.
- **sold:** the s and o hook before ld don't come out as on the plate.

## Changes to other levels

- None. The `nd` and `md` strokes already exist (the brief forms and, end, and Unit 7 want,
  went use `nd`).

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- around, ground, found, count, sound and front drop the ow / short u before n by §124
  (Unit 15: "In the body of a word short u and ow are omitted before n and m"); the plates
  show no hook.
- entry, empty: the initial short e is omitted before the blend (§134), so the outline starts
  with `nd` / `md`. enable keeps a small initial circle on the plate (`e n b`).
- winter: w before a vowel, `u e` (Unit 11), then `nd r`. joint: oi is `o e` (Unit 13).
  signed, blind, filed, wild, child: ī is the broken circle `i`.
- fastened: §51, the s after the a circle belongs to f, so the right s.
- yield: y is written `e` as in Unit 12 (yacht `e o t`); the plate shows a narrow loop.
- held, hold: the h dot before the vowel, as usual.
- exempt: ex is written es (§106), so `e s-right`, then md; the e between s and md is not
  visible.
- convent: con is k (Unit 9).
- Readings to check first on the comparison sheet: prevent (`p r e v nd`), convent, laundry
  (the o hook before nd is small), exempt, stock (`s-right t k`), enable.

## Engine needs

1. **The ld combination (`ld`).** On the plates (old, fold, field, killed, held; p. 74) it
   is the l curve (forward, convex downward) which, instead of ending level, swings up at
   the finish into a short rising tail, ending about 0.5–0.7 of the l's length above the
   line, heading up-right at roughly 60°. Length about that of l plus the tail. Proposed:
   a new stroke `ld` drawn as l followed by an upward turn (one primitive or l + a short
   arc), joined like l (circles before it sit inside the curve, §11).
2. **nd / md lengths.** §134: nd as long as f, md as long as v. Check the existing `nd`
   (2.4 U) and `md` (3.4 U) against the plates (sent, seemed, empty, doomed).
3. **e circle after nd / md at the end** (plenty, empty): the plates show a small circle at
   the top of the upward curve, inside the curve.
4. **Circle before nd / md** (rent, land, planned, grant): the circle sits inside the angle
   above the join, as with the r / l + circle words.

# Manual Unit 20: the method of expressing r (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter VII, Unit 20, printed
pages 88–91 (PDF pages 104–107 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit2.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` / `i`
circles, the `o` / `u` hooks and the `h` dot, written in writing order; vowel marks are
omitted. `a-rev` / `e-rev` are the large / small circle written with **left motion** to add
r (as in Unit 8, §71); `e-rev-s` is that circle made a loop for s (§74). `w-dash` is the
§99 dash under the vowel. Plate crop boxes (80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in
`scratchpad/u20/plates-unit20.json`, ids `word-<word>`. Page 92 (§169 reading and
dictation practice) is connected plates only.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§161.** "A circle or loop is written with the left motion to express r following the
  vowel: Between a downward character, [( ( ) ) / / /] and a forward straight stroke,
  [/ / — —]; compare the following forms:" Right-motion: chat, sham, bin, fame.
  Left-motion: chart, charm, burn, farm. Note: "The circle is placed above the next
  stroke after p, b, as in burn, bird, and below the next stroke in all others, as in
  charm, farm. There is a tendency in rapid writing to curve a straight line when it is
  followed by a circle. Therefore the distinctive method of joining the circle when it is
  written with left motion after straight strokes is adopted to prevent any possibility of
  misreading. Compare germ and bird in the following drill:"
- **§162 Word drill.**
- **§163.** "Between a horizontal and an upward stroke the circle is turned with a left
  motion on the upward stroke to express r following the vowel:"
- **§164.** "Before straight lines, s in ser, cer, sar, and th in ther, thir, may be
  written contrary to the usual method of joining to express r:"
- **§165 R Omitted.** "In many words containing ar, er, or, ir, as in the words large,
  serve, warm, sort, firm, circle, corner, the r is omitted. In applying this principle
  advantage is simply taken of dropping a sound that ordinarily is not stressed in
  speaking."
- **§166 Word drill.** Footnote to modern and eastern: "The syllables tern, dern are
  expressed by ten."
- **§167.** "The termination worthy, as in noteworthy, trustworthy, is expressed by thi,
  and worth by ūth, thus:" noteworthy, trustworthy, Ainsworth.
- **§168 The Syllable Ther.** "The syllable ther, as in either, other, is conveniently
  expressed by the sign for th:" Footnote: "The left-motion th is used in father to
  distinguish this word from faith, which otherwise would have the same form."
- **§169 Reading and dictation practice**: connected text, not transcribed.

**The r circle in tokens.** §161 and §163 extend the Unit 8 left-motion circle (§71,
`a-rev` / `e-rev`, until now only between straight strokes) to two new joinings: after a
downstroke (p, b, f, v, ch, j, sh) before a forward straight stroke (n, m, t, d), and
between a horizontal stroke (k, g, r, l) and an upward one (t, d). The large circle is
used for a, the small for e, i and the obscure ur (as §72 in Unit 8). §165 words drop the
r and use the ordinary circle or hook.

## Left out, and why

- **Same outline as an existing example:** turn (`t e n`, word-tin), born (`b o n`,
  word-bone), court (`k o t`, word-caught), sort (`s-right o t`, word-sought), bother
  (`b o th-under`, word-both).
- **experts (§162):** the s and t after the circle crowd into one small mark.
- **guardian (§163):** a dot inside the final circle (the §120 ia mark), which has no token.
- **courteous (§163):** the ending (e + us, "ʒ") is too small to separate.
- **§164 (desert, discern, concert, concern, insert, inserted, insertion, assert,
  asserted, assertion, search, serge, surgeon, surmise, sermon, third, thirty,
  thermometer):** the rule is that s / th is written "contrary to the usual method"; at
  plate size it can't be decided which s (or th) form the "6"-shaped sign is against the
  §49 / §78 rules, so the outlines would be guesses. See "Engine needs" 3.
- **reverse, reserve, endorse, surprise, surplus, orchestra (§166):** the s signs and
  circles on the downstrokes are too small to read reliably.
- **north, northern, warmth, southern, worth, worthy, worry, source (§166):** which th
  form, or the hook / circle order, can't be read with confidence.
- **assortment, nervous, services (§166):** the circle and s signs crowd together.
- **§167 noteworthy, trustworthy, Ainsworth:** the thi / ūth endings are too small, and
  Ainsworth carries a capital mark under it.
- **hitherto (§168):** the ending after th is unclear.
- **§169:** connected reading practice only.
- **The 33 r-circle words in "Words needing new marks":** the §161 / §163 left-motion circle beside a curve or before an upward stroke: the engine draws it, but not on the side the manual's note and the plates put it (above the next stroke after p, b; below it otherwise; hanging below a horizontal stroke before t, d), so these wait until that placement is built.

## Changes to other levels

- None. §165 confirms the readings of existing words with an omitted r, and the five
  duplicates above show those outlines already in use for other words (the manual
  accepts the homographs).

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- Circle size follows the vowel as in Unit 8 (§72): the obscure ur / er / ir take the
  small circle (burn, bird, germ, fern, shirt, curt), a and ar the large (barn, chart,
  cart, guard).
- A final -er after a straight stroke (barter, burner, charter, farmer, girder, corner,
  border, quarter, larger) is read as the small left-motion circle, as the plates show a
  small circle turned the §71 way at the end.
- farmers ends in the §74 s loop (`e-rev-s`).
- In §166 the r is dropped and the ordinary circle or hook is written (large: `l a j`;
  cord, board: `o` hook).
- The §168 th follows §78: the left-motion `th-under` before / after o, r, l (brother,
  leather) and in father by the footnote; elsewhere the right-motion `th-over`.
- quarter carries the §99 w dash (visible under the plate).
- Least certain readings, to check first on the comparison sheet: fertile (`f e-rev t l`:
  the t is very short), chairman, inert, circle, surface, service, storm, warrant.

## Engine needs

1. **§161: the left-motion circle after a downstroke** (p, b, f, v, ch, j, sh, and the s
   before p in spurt, spared) before a forward straight stroke (n, m, t, d) or a blend
   beginning like one (ten, nd). Today `a-rev` / `e-rev` are allowed only between two
   straight strokes. The manual's note fixes the side: "The circle is placed above the
   next stroke after p, b, as in burn, bird, and below the next stroke in all others, as
   in charm, farm." On the plates burn / barn / bird show the circle sitting on top of the
   n / d at the foot of the b ("6" with the line leaving from the circle's top), farm /
   fern / chart the circle below the m / t.
2. **§163: the left-motion circle between a horizontal stroke (k, g, r, l) and an upward
   one (t, d, ten)**: "the circle is turned with a left motion on the upward stroke".
   On the plates (cart, card, guard, lard, alert) the circle hangs below the end of the
   k / g / l and the t / d leaves from it upward to the right, so the circle sits in the
   angle on the upward stroke's side.
3. **§164 (left out):** a way to state "contrary" s / th joinings once read from larger
   plates; the tokens `s-left` / `s-right`, `th-over` / `th-under` already exist, so only
   the reading is missing.
4. **Final small left-motion circle after t, d, n, m** (barter, charter, border, corner):
   existing §71 behaviour; check its size against the plates.

# Manual Unit 18: frequent word-beginnings and phrasing (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter VI, Unit 18, printed
pages 80–83 (PDF pages 96–99 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit2.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` / `i`
circles, the `o` / `u` hooks and the `h` dot, written in writing order; vowel marks are
omitted. Plate crop boxes (80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in
`scratchpad/u18/plates-unit18.json`, ids `word-<word>`, `brief-<first word>` and
`phrase-<words joined by ->`. Pages 82–83 (§151 reading and dictation practice, §152
writing practice) are connected plates and longhand sentences only.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§145 Frequent word-beginnings.** "The vowel is omitted in the syllables be, de, re,
  dis, and mis:"
- **§146.** "The vowel is retained when de precedes k, g, as in decay, degrade."
- **§147.** "The vowel in re is omitted only before a downward character, as in replace,
  repent, review, repair, resign, reception."
- **§148 Phrasing principles.** "The word had when following a pronoun is expressed as
  shown in the following illustrations:" I had, he had, they had, we had, you had.
- **§149.** "The phrases was-not and is-not are expressed easily and legibly by using the
  blending principle:" was not, he was not, it is not, it was not, there was not, there is
  not. Note: "If the contractions wasn't, isn't, etc. need to be positively indicated, the
  apostrophe is placed above the forms."
- **§150 Brief forms for common words.**
- **§151 Reading and dictation practice; §152 Writing practice**: connected text, not
  transcribed.

**The prefixes in tokens.** be = `b`, de = `d`, re = `r`, mis = `m s-left`, dis =
`d s-left` (the d with a small left s at its top: the s belongs to the syllable dis, after
d, so §49 gives the left s).

## Words needing new marks

None proposed. The left-out items below need readings, not new marks, except mishap (an h
dot within the outline) and the §149 apostrophe, which is optional.

## Left out, and why

- **Same outline as an existing example:** below (`b l o`, word-blow); he had (`h e d`,
  word-head); we had (`u e d`, word-wed); you had (the oo hook and d, `u d`, brief-word,
  with a dot over it on the plate that the engine has no reading for).
- **Not clear enough on the plate to read safely:**
  - beneath: the end after b n is a tiny circle and stroke (e th?), too small to tell th
    over from under.
  - besides: b, a small s, the broken circle, d and s run into one knot.
  - dislike, disgrace: the joins after the dis s (l i k; g r a s) are too compressed.
  - review, revise: the v runs into the ū / ī and final s and the ends can't be read
    stroke by stroke.
  - misery: m, s, then a circle tangled with r and the final e.
  - they had: a tiny curl (th?) before the circle and d; the over / under form can't be
    told.
  - §149 was not, he was not, it is not, it was not, there was not, there is not: the s
    and the nt blend run together; which s sign, and whether a circle is written, can't
    be told at print size.
  - Brief forms advertise, previous, occasion, quantity, hundred, improve / -ment,
    acquaint / -ance, sufficient, merchandise, determine, pleasure, catalogue: the plates
    are small and several strokes run together (hundred also has a dot).
- **debate:** the d drawn too long against the plate's short d; left out until the be/de prefixes before b are measured.
- **mishap:** an h dot written to the right of the p, within the outline; no token places
  the h dot after a stroke.
- **§146 decay, degrade; §147:** named in the rules but not printed as outlines.

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- **dis and mis take the left s** (§49 after d and m). The plates show only a small tick
  at the top of the d, consistent with the left s.
- **s between strokes** follows §51: deceit, decision, resign and reason take the right s
  (before t, sh, n); reception the left s (before p).
- **depart** is d with the brief form part (`p t`); repent and depend end in the nd blend
  (§133).
- **reform** is r with the brief form form (`f m`); no r is visible between f and m.
- **discover**: the outline ends in the v; no r is visible after it.
- **dispel** shows no circle between p and l, so it is `d s-left p l`.
- **decision**: the "9" after the s is read as the e circle and sh (zh), with no n.
- **record** shows r and a long d with no k; **educate** is e, d, and a short k curling at
  the top of the d; **difficult** is the def blend (Unit 17) and k.
- **I had** is the broken circle and d.
- Least certain readings, to check first on the comparison sheet: deceit, decision,
  discover, reception, reform, educate, difficult.

## Engine needs

1. **dis: a small left s at the top of d.** The plates show it as a tiny tick where the d
   turns into the next stroke; check that `d s-left` followed by p, k, m and l draws a
   small enough s and doesn't open a loop.
2. **Circle then downstroke (reception, replace, depress):** ordinary §11–18 joins.
3. Nothing new is needed for the transcribed outlines; all compile with the current
   engine.

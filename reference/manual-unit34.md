# Manual Unit 34: states and territories; principal cities (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter XII, Unit 34, printed
pages 146–150 (PDF pages 162–166 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit32.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` / `i`
circles, the `o` / `u` hooks, the `h` dot and the `w-dash`, written in writing order; vowel
marks are omitted. Plate crop boxes (80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in
`scratchpad/u34/plates-unit34.json`, ids `word-<slug of the title>`; they cover the Words
table only. Page 150 (§240) is reading and dictation practice.

The outlines carry no capital mark on these plates: the names are written as plain outlines.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§238 States and territories.** "The abbreviations used in the following list are those
  adopted by the Post Office Department:" The list: Ala., Alaska, Ariz., Ark., Calif., Colo.,
  Conn., Del., D. C., Fla., Ga., Guam, Hawaii, Idaho, Ill., Ind., Iowa, Kans., Ky., La.,
  Maine, Md., Mass., Mich., Minn., Miss., Mo., Mont., Nebr., Nev., N. H., N. J., N. Mex.,
  N. Y., N. C., N. Dak., Ohio, Okla., Oreg., Pa., P. I., P. R., R. I., S. C., S. Dak., Tenn.,
  Tex., Utah, Vt., Va., Wash., W. Va., Wis., Wyo.
- **§239 Principal cities of the United States.** "The following names of cities are
  arranged in the order of their population:" 93 cities, New York to Salt Lake City and
  Tacoma (pp. 147–149).
- **§240 Reading and dictation practice**: connected text, not transcribed.

## Words needing new marks

None proposed. The names left out below are left out for their readings, not for a missing
placement, except the lone-circle outlines (Iowa) and the h-dot forms noted there.

## Left out, and why

- **N. J.** (`n j`): on the plate the n is written through the j, not joined to it.
- **Same outline as an existing example:** Ga. (`j a`, = word-jay), Ill. (`e l`, =
  phrase-he-will), Kans. (`k`), Ky. (`k e`, = word-key), La. (`l a`, = word-lay), Maine
  (`m a n`, = word-main), Nebr. (`n b`, = brief-unable), Nev. (`n v`, = brief-never), Pa.
  (`p a`, = word-pay), R. I. (`r i`, = brief-right), Colo. (`k o l`, = word-coal).
- **Lone circle or lone hook:** Iowa (a circle alone), Ohio (a hook alone).
- **h dot before or under the outline:** Hawaii, N. H.
- **Can't be read as tokens without guessing:** Alaska, Ariz., Ark., Conn., D. C., Guam,
  Ind., Mass., Minn., Miss., Mont., N. Mex. (whether `n m` or a single stroke), N. Y.,
  N. C., Oreg., P. R., S. C., S. Dak. (the s direction), Tenn. (one smooth curve, not `t n`
  with an angle), Tex., Utah, Wash., W. Va., Wis., Wyo.
- **§239 cities, all but Omaha and Erie:** the outlines are long and run several syllables
  together (New York, Chicago, Philadelphia, Los Angeles and the rest); at the plates'
  size they can't be split into tokens without guessing. Some also need the ing dot
  (Reading) or an h dot (New Haven, Hartford, Houston).
- **§240:** reading practice only.

## Inference

- Calif. as `k a l`: the plate writes only "Cal", the l running on past the circle.
- Idaho as `i d o`: the broken circle, the upward d, and a small o hook at the d's top.
- N. Dak. as `n d a k`: a short n, the upward d, the circle at its top and the k curving
  over and down.
- Okla. as `o k l a`: a small hook, the k, the l and the closing circle.
- Omaha as `o m a`: a small hook before the m, the circle at its end.
- Erie as `e r e`: two small circles joined by a short r.

## Engine needs

None for the words above. Iowa and Ohio would need a lone circle and a lone hook; Hawaii
and N. H. an h dot placed beside a stroke.

# Manual Unit 19: the ten-den and tem-dem blends (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter VII, Unit 19, printed
pages 84–87 (PDF pages 100–103 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit2.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` / `i`
circles, the `o` / `u` hooks and the `h` dot, written in writing order; vowel marks are
omitted. This unit uses the blend strokes already in `app/data/strokes.js`: `ten` (ten,
den) and `tem` (tem, dem), both upward curves "(" bending forward at the top; `ten` is the
shorter. Plate crop boxes (80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in
`scratchpad/u19/plates-unit19.json`, ids `word-<word>` and `phrase-<first words>`. Page 87
(§160 reading and dictation practice) is connected plates only.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§153.** "By blending t or d with n or m facile blends result, which make possible the
  writing of many syllables with but one movement of the pen:" t + n "blended becomes" one
  curve "and expresses ten, den"; t + m "blended becomes" one curve "and expresses tem,
  dem".
- **§154 Word drill.**
- **§155.** "The blend is not employed when a strongly accented vowel or diphthong occurs
  in the syllable. Such words as dean, dine, team, tame, dome, dime, and other words of one
  syllable are written in full. The syllable tain, as in maintain, attain, however, is
  expressed by ten."
- **§156.** "Where it is possible to use either ten-den or ent-end, as in intention, the
  right-motion blend is given preference."
- **§157 Phrasing principles.** "The blending principle makes possible some interesting
  and valuable phrases:"
- **§158.** "When do-not is preceded by a pronoun, it is expressed by the sign den:"
- **§159.** "When necessary, don't may be distinguished from do not by writing don for
  don't, thus:" I don't, we don't, they don't.
- **§160 Reading and dictation practice**: connected text, not transcribed.

## Left out, and why

- **Same outline as another example of this unit:** retain is merged with written
  (`r e ten`), continuous with continues (`k ten u s-right`); the plates are the same.
  at once (`ten s-left`) has the outline of the word dense, and is left out.
- **Plate too small, tangled or ambiguous to read stroke by stroke:** threaten (the first
  strokes), danger, evidence, distance, residence, tonight (it looks the same as deny on
  the plate; the t is not visible), sustain, captain, obtain (the o hook is not visible
  before the b), audience, random, attempt, bottom, wisdom, victim, attendance, tendency.
- **Phrases:** to make, it must be, in due course, in due time, what to do, to draw
  (§157), I do not see, they do not, they do not know (§158): the joinings are too small to
  read with confidence.
- **§159 don't** (I don't, we don't, they don't): the o hook and n after den are too small
  to separate on the plate.
- **§160:** connected reading practice only.

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- The s signs follow §49/§51: sudden and sentence begin with the right s (before d / a
  circle); dense, condense, tennis and items end with the left s (after n / m).
- con- is k (Unit 9), as in continue, contain, condense, continent.
- -tion / -sion after the blend is sh (extension, intention).
- tenant and continent end with the nd blend (ten + nt), the reverse curve on the plates.
- -tain is ten (§155): attain, detain, contain, maintain (men + ten), bulletin, button,
  cotton, written.
- able is b (Unit 15): attainable.
- I in the §158 phrases is the broken circle `i`; we is the w loop (`u e`), you the oo
  hook `u`; believe is `b` and know is `o` (the n is absorbed by den), as the plates show.
- Least certain readings, to check first on the comparison sheet: broaden, tender,
  extension, sentence, itemize, academy, estimate, seldom, I do not know, you do not know.

## Engine needs

1. **Check the `ten` and `tem` blends** against the plates: §153 shows ten about the
   length of the nd blend and tem about the length of md; both start nearly vertical and
   bend forward at the top. They are not marked `verify` in strokes.js, but no earlier
   unit has used them as words.
2. **A circle at the top of a blend** (deny, at any, to me, to my, to meet, to mean): the
   circle joins the forward-bending end, inside the curve by §11.
3. **A circle before a blend** (attain, item, written, detain, I do not): the circle is on
   the outside of the blend's start on the plates (a "6" shape).
4. **The w loop before den** (we do not, sweeten) and the oo hook before it (you do not,
   button) use the Unit 10–13 joinings.

# Manual Unit 12: y, ng and nk, prefixes and suffixes, phrases (source transcription for level 17)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter IV, Unit 12, printed
pages 56–60 (PDF pages 72–76 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit7.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` circles, the
§71 `a-rev` / `e-rev` circles and the `h` dot, in writing order; vowel marks are omitted.
`o` is the o hook. Plate crop boxes (80 dpi, printed pages) are in
`scratchpad/u12/plates-unit12.json`. Pages 59–60 (§110 reading, §111 writing practice) are
connected text only. Plates were read at 300–900 dpi.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§104 Method of expressing Y.** "Y has the sound of long e, as in yacht, yoke, and when
  followed by a hook vowel is expressed by the small circle. Ye, as in year, yet, is
  expressed by a small loop; ya, by a large loop."
- **§105 The signs for ng and nk.** "The sound ng, as in ring, rang, is expressed by n
  written at a slightly downward slant; nk (sounded ngk), as in bank, rank, is expressed
  by a longer stroke on the same slant." Tokens: `ng`, `nk`.
- **§106 Prefixes and suffixes.** "The vowel is omitted in the prefixes en, in, un, em, im
  when the prefix is followed by a consonant; when a written vowel follows the prefix, the
  initial vowel is retained. Ex is expressed by es. The suffix ings is expressed by a left
  s and ingly by a small circle substituted for the ing-dot."
- **§107.** "Negative words beginning with in, un, im in which the n or m is doubled are
  distinguished from the positive forms by omitting one of the doubled consonants and
  inserting the initial vowel": known / unknown, noticed / unnoticed, necessary /
  unnecessary.
- **§108 Frequent phrases.**
- **§109 Brief forms for common words.**
- **§110 Reading and dictation practice, §111 Writing practice** (pp. 59–60): connected
  text, not transcribed.

## Left out, and why

- **Not legible enough to transcribe:** unseen (the s sign after n), indeed (whether d e d
  or one long stroke), embrace and emphasis (the end of the outline, s or circle),
  examine, excess, expense (the ex = es joining and the circle sizes), necessary /
  unnecessary, unnoticed (also needs the u hook).
- **§108 phrases:** of them, to ask, into the, does not, in reply, that they, did not (an
  element not yet introduced or unclear at print size); if you, we would, we should,
  we may, with us, we must, will you, through the, for us, who have, as you (the u hook
  for you / we / who / us); over the (a disjoined sign written above).
- **§109 brief forms:** young (the start is a tiny mark, hook or circle unclear), thank
  (a dot after the stroke), strength / strong, communicate / communication, character,
  friend / friendly, return, experience, recent (joinings or s signs not clear enough).
- **known:** already a Unit 7 example (`n o n`); it is printed here only for contrast.
- **§110–111:** connected text.

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- §104 "followed by a hook vowel is expressed by the small circle": yacht, yawn, yoke are
  the e circle joined to the o hook. The plates show the circle and the hook, with the
  hook turned before n (yawn) as by §67.
- s signs: sing, sank, songs (initial) take the right s, as §49 rule 2 (before a circle
  or hook); spring takes the left s before p; the final s of songs (after ng) and of
  answer (after n) and else / list (after l) is the left s; the plates agree.
- ng and nk are the existing `ng` / `nk` strokes; circle sizes follow the vowel groups and
  agree with the plates (rang, rank, sank, frank, bank, blank, banquet large; ring, drink,
  king, spring small).
- The ye loop lies along the stroke that follows it; whether the r of yard / yarn is
  omitted or blended into the loop is an inference from the plates, which show only the
  loop and d / n.

## Engine needs

- **The ye and ya loops (§104).** A narrow loop at the beginning of an outline, lying
  along the direction of the stroke that follows and running into it: yet (a small loop
  then t up to the right, p. 56), year (a small loop then r), yellow, yes (the loop on
  its own, upright, with s); ya is the same loop larger: yard (a large loop then d),
  yarn (a large loop then n). It looks like the §74 s loop written at the start instead
  of the end. Proposed tokens `ye`, `ya`; sizes to be measured from p. 56.
- **The ingly circle and the ings s (§106).** Written in place of the ing dot, i.e.
  disjoined, close to the end of the word: lovingly, seemingly, exceedingly (a small
  circle below / beside the last stroke); meetings, savings, evenings (a small left s
  below the end). Proposed tokens `ingly` (disjoined small circle) and `ings`
  (disjoined left s), placed like the ing dot.
- **The u (oo) hook** for initial w, un, you, we, who (wing, unknown, the phrases with you
  and we): the hook stroke `u` exists but its joinings are not verified.
- **Dot after a stroke** (thank) and the ing dot, and **disjoined over** written above
  (over the): marks placed apart from the outline.

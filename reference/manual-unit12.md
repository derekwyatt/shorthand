# Manual Unit 12: y, ng and nk, prefixes and suffixes (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter IV, Unit 12, printed
pages 56–60 (PDF pages 72–76 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit2.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` circles, the
`o` / `u` hooks and the `h` dot, written in writing order; vowel marks are omitted. Plate
crop boxes (80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in `scratchpad/u12/plates-unit12.json`, ids
`word-<word>`, `brief-<first word>`, `phrase-<slug>`. Pages 59–60 (§110 reading, §111
writing practice) are text and connected plates only.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§104 Method of expressing Y.** "Y has the sound of long e, as in yacht, yoke, and when
  followed by a hook vowel is expressed by the small circle. Ye, as in year, yet, is
  expressed by a small loop; ya, by a large loop."
- **§105 The signs for ng and nk.** "The sound ng, as in ring, rang, is expressed by n
  written at a slightly downward slant; nk (sounded ngk), as in bank, rank, is expressed
  by a longer stroke on the same slant."
- **§106 Prefixes and suffixes.** "The vowel is omitted in the prefixes en, in, un, em, im
  when the prefix is followed by a consonant; when a written vowel follows the prefix, the
  initial vowel is retained. Ex is expressed by es. The suffix ings is expressed by a left
  s and ingly by a small circle substituted for the ing-dot."
- **§107.** "Negative words beginning with in, un, im in which the n or m is doubled are
  distinguished from the positive forms by omitting one of the doubled consonants and
  inserting the initial vowel."
- **§108 Frequent phrases.**
- **§109 Brief forms for common words.**

## Left out, and why

- **known (§107):** same outline as `word-known` (Unit 10).
- **does not (§108):** `d u s-right n` is the outline of `word-dozen`.
- **embrace, emphasis, expense, examine, excess (§106):** the endings (circle against
  s, the es prefix circle and its s) are too compressed on the plate to transcribe safely.
- **noticed, unnoticed, necessary, unnecessary (§107):** the circle–s–t ending and the
  ses blend are too small to read.
- **§108:** of them, to ask, if you, we would, we should, we may, we must, with us, into
  the, in reply, that they, through the (the "we" loop form, th forms, or junctions not
  legible); which have and who have (the plates overlap each other); as you (the s and
  hook are not separable).
- **§109:** young (the y sign is not clear), strength / strong, communicate, character,
  friend / friendly, return, experience, recent (the joinings or the minor marks are not
  clear enough to transcribe).

## Engine needs

- **ng / nk slant (§105):** the plates write ng and nk on a slight downward slant, nk about
  twice as long. The existing `ng` / `nk` strokes are used as they are; check their slant
  against the plates (ring, rank, bank) on the comparison sheet.
- **y as a circle before a hook (yacht, yawn, yoke, youth):** the e circle before `o` /
  `u` is joined at the start of the hook. The `o` hook after a circle with no stroke
  before it is new; check it reads.
- **u hook in youth, for us, will you:** depends on the Unit 10 `u` joinings (§93 rounded
  join into `s-right`, as in for us).
- **ted after n (indeed):** an angle n → ted.
- **New marks** for the words above (ye / ya loops, ingly circle, ings s) are not needed
  for these 47 examples.

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- songs: the initial s is read as `s-right` (s before a hook vowel, §49 rule 2) and the
  final as `s-left` (s after n); the plate's final curl agrees.
- wing and wrong use the `u` / `o` hooks for w and o, as the plates show a hook.
- at all: written t with the o hook ("all"), as on the plate.
- effort: the plate's small top circle is read as the initial e.
- unknown: the §107 rule (one n dropped, the initial vowel inserted) gives u + n o n; the
  plate shows the hook joined to the first n.

# Manual Unit 8: the method of expressing r (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter III, Unit 8, printed
pages 39–41 (PDF pages 55–57 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit4.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` circles, the
`h` dot and the `o` hook, in writing order; vowel marks are omitted. Plate crop boxes
(80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in `scratchpad/u8/plates-unit8.json`, ids `word-<word>`
and `brief-<first word>`. Page 41 (§77) is connected reading practice only.

Almost every word in this unit uses a joining the engine cannot draw yet: the
**left-motion (reversed) circle on straight strokes that expresses r**. Those words are in
"Words needing the new joining" below, written with proposed tokens; see "Engine needs".

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§71.** "The circle is written with left motion to express r following the vowel:
  1. Before and after straight strokes: [art, mar, arch, share]
  2. Between straight strokes in the same direction: [tart, dared, church, murmur]"
- **§72.** "It is generally more facile to use the circle for the obscure vowel sound heard
  in ur as in church, murmur, urge, hurt."
- **§73 Frequent-word drill.**
- **§74 S following a left-motion circle.** "The letter s is added to a final left-motion
  circle on straight strokes by changing the circle to a loop:" tears, shares, stairs,
  dares, nears, manners.
- **§75 Brief forms for common words.**
- **§76.** "After abbreviated words and words ending in a left-motion circle on straight
  strokes, a disjoined r expresses er, or, thus:" keeper, dearer, worker. "When the forms
  are distinctive, the r is joined, thus:" greater, bigger, smaller. "When a brief form
  ends with the last consonant of a word, the left motion circle is used to express or, er
  after straight strokes:" sooner, former.
- **§77 Reading and dictation practice** (p. 41): connected text, not transcribed.

## Engine needs

**The left-motion r circle (§71).** On straight strokes the ordinary circle is written with
right motion (clockwise, §12, §17). To express r after the vowel, the same circle is
written with **left motion** (counter-clockwise). The plates show it:
- At the end of a straight stroke: mar, near, mere, manner (n/m: the circle sits on top of
  the line's end, turned up), tear, dare (t/d: the circle at the top end, on the upper-left
  side), share, chair, jar (sh/ch/j: the circle at the bottom end, opening on the right).
- At the beginning: art, earn, arm, urge, arch (the circle before the stroke, on the side
  opposite to the §12 circle).
- Between straight strokes in the same direction: tart, dared, church, murmur (one circle,
  turned the other way from §17).
- After a forward stroke and a circle or hook: better, later, motor, teacher, cashier.
- The rule is only for straight strokes; on curves the circle keeps its §11 side.

**The r loop with s (§74).** A final left-motion circle on a straight stroke becomes a
narrow loop along the stroke to add s: tears, dares, nears, manners, shares, stairs. The
loop is about the circle's width and twice as long, lying along the stroke's direction.

**Disjoined r (§76):** keeper, dearer, worker write r separately, close to the outline;
a disjoined sign.

## Left out, and why

- **sister, chapter, minister (§73):** the small s and the circles crowd together on the
  plate; the s sign and the circle order cannot be read safely.
- **either, rather (§75):** th with a circle whose motion is not clear; **above** (a big
  hook then the b–v blend, unclear), **capital** (p or b after k unclear), **necessary**,
  **yesterday**, **together**, **prepare**, **subject**: joinings not clear enough at print
  size, or y / hooks the engine lacks.
- **keeper, dearer, worker (§76):** disjoined r.
- **greater, smaller, sooner (§76):** their brief forms (great, small, soon) have not been
  introduced in the transcribed units, and the joined r is too small to read.
- **§77 reading practice:** connected text.

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- Circle size (a or e) is read from the plate; it agrees with the vowel group, with the
  obscure ur taking the small circle (§72).
- `real / regard` is written `r e`: the circle is on a curve, so it is an ordinary §11
  circle; the plate shows it inside r. That outline may coincide with an existing
  example; check before adding.
- **love** shows no u hook on the plate: l joined directly to v.
- **opinion** is `o p n`: the o hook at the top runs into the p, with n at the bottom. The
  manual states no rule for a hook before a downstroke; the plate shows no angle there.

# Manual Unit 4: the downward characters, pr pl br bl fr fl, brief forms (source transcription for level 9)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter II, Unit 4, printed
pages 18–22 (PDF pages 34–38 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

This file is the source of truth for the Unit 4 words and lesson in `app/data/units/`.
Conventions are as in `manual-unit2.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` circles and
the `h` dot; vowel marks are omitted. The circle size (a or e) is read from the plate.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§34.** "The characters for the consonants in this chapter are derived from another
  elliptical figure." Letters and signs: P, B, F, V, CH, J, SH, S (two signs). Word signs:
  put (p); be, by, but (b); for (f); have (v); which, change (ch); shall, ship (sh); is,
  his (s). "Left motion: P B S. Right motion: F V S." Tokens: `p b f v ch j sh s-left
  s-right`.
- **§35.** "All these characters are written downward. Ch is named chay, and sh is named
  ish. The signs for sh and s are very small. As s is one of the most frequent sounds in
  the language, two signs are provided for it to facilitate joining in various
  combinations."
- **§36.** "Many of the consonants follow each other consecutively; for example, r and l
  frequently follow p, b, as in play, brave. As skill in writing such combinations is
  essential to speed and accuracy, the following movement drills should be practiced
  until fluency is secured."
- **§37.** "Pr and Pl. In writing pr and pl, start to the left."
- **§38.** "Br and Bl. In writing br and bl, start down."
- **§39.** "Fr and Fl. In writing the combinations fr and fl, the angle is rounded to give
  fluency. The motion is just the same as in writing a part of the longhand y."
- **§40 Frequent-word drill.** Outlines only, no sound spellings.
- **§41 Brief forms.** Footnote: "The sign for the prefix over written above a following
  character is used to express the word over."
- **§42.** "As a prefix, after is expressed by af. In compounds, every is expressed by ev."
- **§43.** "The word been following have, has, had is phrased and is expressed by b."
- **§44 Business abbreviations.** Dear Madam, Very truly yours, Yours very truly.
- **§45 Reading and dictation practice** (p. 22): connected text only, not transcribed.

The §34 word signs put, be / by, for, have, which, shall, is / his are already level 1
brief forms; §34 adds **but** (b), **change** (ch) and **ship** (sh) to them.

## Left out, and why

- **Drill words with the outline of an existing example:** if (`e f`), age (`a j`),
  each (`e ch`), she (`sh e`), happy (`h a p e`), all level 4; play (§40) repeats §37.
- **one / won (§41):** written with the u hook (`u n`); hooks are left out, as in Unit 3.
- **over (§41):** the o hook written above the following character, a disjoined sign.
- **most (§41):** m and a tiny s; the plate does not show clearly enough which s sign
  (left or right motion) is used.
- **has been (§43):** the small sign at the top of the b is not clear enough to tell s
  from a circle.
- **Very truly yours, Yours very truly (§44):** small joined marks at the ends of the
  strokes that cannot be read reliably at print size.
- **§45 reading practice:** connected text, not a word list.

## Changes to other levels

- **but, change, ship** join the existing brief forms b, ch, sh (titles become
  "be / by / but", "which / change", "shall / ship").
- **ever** (`e v`) is the outline of the level 4 word "eve". The brief form is the
  manual's own; it lives in a different level, so a quiz never offers both for one outline.
- **every** (`e v e`) and **people** (`p e p`) are new outlines; no existing word uses them.

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- §37–38: p and b flow into r and l without a visible angle on the plates ("start to the
  left" / "start down" describe where the pen begins the p or b). `join.js` joins them
  end-to-start like any other pair, so pr, pl, br, bl may show a slight angle where the
  manual has none.
- §39: `join.js` rounds fr, fl (and vr, vl by inference) with `ROUND_PAIRS`.
- had been: the plate shows no h dot over the circle; the outline is transcribed as
  printed (`a d b`).
- braided: the final stroke is read as the `ted` blend (§26) from its length on the plate.

# Manual Unit 26: the abbreviating principle, continued; vowel omitted before shun (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter IX, Unit 26, printed
pages 113–115 (PDF pages 129–131 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit2.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` / `i`
circles, the `o` / `u` hooks, the `h` dot and the `w-dash`, written in writing order; vowel
marks are omitted. The shun termination is written with `sh` (Unit 6). Plate crop boxes
(80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in `scratchpad/u26/plates-unit26.json`, ids
`word-<word>`. Page 115 (§200) is reading and dictation practice.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§198.** "Write through the consonant following the accented syllable, if writing
  through the accented syllable does not give a sufficiently distinctive form." "To
  illustrate, writing ab for the word absent would not be sufficiently distinctive, but by
  writing abs, the word is immediately suggested. In context, at would not suggest
  attitude or attribute, but atit and atrib would furnish perfectly legible forms:"
- **§199 Omission of Vowel Before "Shun".** "The vowel is omitted in the terminations
  tition, tation, dition, dation, nition, nation, mission, mation:"
- **§200 Reading and dictation practice**: connected text, not transcribed.

## Left out, and why

- **Same outline as an existing example:** render (`r e nd` = word-rent), total (`t o t`
  = word-taught).
- **Too small or tangled on the plate to read without guessing (§198):** absolute (the
  ending after the s can't be told from absent's), appreciate / appreciation, association,
  benefit, cancel, enthusiasm, essential, journal, reciprocate, numerous, ordinary, specify,
  prominent, practice, separate, social, territory.
- **Too small or tangled on the plate (§199):** repetition, competition, consolidation,
  combination, recognition, destination.
- **§200:** connected reading practice only.

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- The shun termination is written `sh`, as in Unit 6; §199 drops the vowel before it, so
  the sh joins directly to the t, d, n or m.
- con- and com- are written `k` (Unit 9): condition `k d sh`, commission `k m sh`.
- quotation carries the §99 w dash under the o hook, as on the plate.
- permanent ends in the m (the -ent is cut off, §198); the plate shows one long straight
  line after the pr, read as m.
- intimation: the rising curve after the n is read as the tem blend.

## Engine needs

None new. To check first on the comparison sheet: the sh ending after an upward t or d

readings I'm least sure of along with similar and frequent.

# Manual Unit 27: compound words; figures (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter IX, Unit 27, printed
pages 116–120 (PDF pages 132–136 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit2.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` / `i`
circles, the `o` / `u` hooks, the `h` dot and the `w-dash`, written in writing order; vowel
marks are omitted. Plate crop boxes (80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in
`scratchpad/u27/plates-unit27.json`, ids `word-<word>`. Page 119 (§207) is reading and
dictation practice and page 120 (§208) is writing practice.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§201 Compound Words.** "A number of compounds may be obtained by joining brief forms:"
- **§202 Key to Compound Words.** The key lists the compounds by their first part: any,
  be, ever-y, here, there, where, soever, some, with. "Note: Slight modifications or
  omissions are made in the forms for anywhere, anyhow, hereinafter, herewith, however,
  sometime, somewhere, and the compounds beginning with every. These should receive special
  attention. The form for notwithstanding is not-with-s."
- **§203 Irregular Compounds**: meanwhile, otherwise, thanksgiving.
- **§204 Figures, etc.** "After numerals the word dollars is expressed by d; hundred by n
  placed under the numeral; thousand by th; million by m placed on the line close to the
  numeral; billion by b; pounds (weight or money) by p; gallons by g; barrels by br; bushels
  by bsh; feet by f; francs by fr; cwt. by nw; o'clock by o placed over the numeral:"
  Footnote: "The sign for hundred is placed beneath the figure to distinguish it
  positively from million, which is written beside the figure."
- **§205.** "The above signs may be used after the article a and such words as per, few,
  several:"
- **§206.** "Cents when preceded by dollars may be expressed by writing the figures
  representing them very small and above the numerals for the dollars; when not preceded
  by dollars, the sign for s is placed above the figures. Per cent is expressed by s written
  below the figures; per cent per annum by adding n to per cent."
- **§207 Reading and dictation practice** and **§208 Writing practice**: connected text,
  not transcribed.

## Left out, and why

- **Too small or tangled on the plate to read without guessing (§201):** anybody, anyone,
  anyhow, anyway; beforehand, behindhand, beside, besides; whatever, whichever, however,
  whoever, everybody, everyone, everywhere; hereafter, hereinafter, hereinbefore, hereto,
  heretofore, hereunto, herewith; thereafter, thereto; whereabouts, whereas; all five
  soever compounds (whatsoever, wheresoever, whensoever, whosoever, whomsoever); somehow,
  sometime, somewhat, somewhere.
- **Built on brief forms the app does not have yet (with, what):** therewith, within,
  withstand, forthwith, notwithstanding (and whatever above).
- **§203 irregular compounds:** meanwhile, otherwise, thanksgiving: the plate forms are
  irregular abbreviations that can't be read stroke by stroke with confidence.
- **§204–§206 figures:** written with numerals, which the writing pad does not take.
- **§207, §208:** connected reading and writing practice only.

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- Each compound is read as its brief forms joined: any `n e`, be `b`, ever `e v`, when
  `e n`, here `h e r`, there `th-under`, where `a r`, else `l s-left`, some `s-right m`,
  for `f`, from `f m`, upon `p n`, in `n`, on `o n`, long `l ng`.
- anywhere is one of the forms the note says are modified: the plate shows n joined straight
  to the large circle of where, so the e of any is dropped (`n a r`).
- whereof: of is the o hook alone, as in the phrases of it, of which (Unit 10).
- body in somebody is written b alone; one in someone as `u n`, as in the phrase one or two.
- belong: the b runs into a nearly flat l before the ng.
- elsewhere: the dip before the circle is read as the s of else.

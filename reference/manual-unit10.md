# Manual Unit 10: the oo hook (source transcription for level 15)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter IV, Unit 10, printed
pages 48–51 (PDF pages 64–67 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit2.md` and `manual-unit7.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus
the circles and the `h` dot, in writing order; vowel marks (the dot under ŏŏ, the tick
under ōō) are omitted, so words that differ only by a mark share one outline. `u` is the
oo hook. Plate crop boxes (80 dpi, printed pages) are in `scratchpad/u10/plates-unit10.json`.
Page 51 (reading practice) is connected text only.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§91.** "The upper part of the small elliptical figure [ellipse], which is called the
  oo-hook, is used to express the following sounds:" ŭ as in tuck, ŏŏ as in took, ōō as in
  tomb. "Key to Vowel Sounds: The duck took to the pool."
- **§92 Frequent-word drill.**
- **§93.** "The combination us is written without an angle at the beginning of words, or
  when it follows a downstroke or k, g, as in us, shoes, campus, cousin, etc."
- **§94 The oo-hook Modified.** "To avoid an unnecessary angle, the oo-hook is turned under
  after n, m. It is also turned under after k or g if followed by r or l:"
- **§95 Brief forms for common words.**
- **Reading and dictation practice** (p. 51): connected text, not transcribed.

## Left out, and why

- **The duck took to the pool (§91):** the key sentence; duck and pool are not printed
  separately, and phrasing "to the" is not this unit's topic.
- **shoes, campus (§93):** named in the rule text but not printed as plates.
- **carry (§95):** a large circle with a second, small loop inside it at the end of k; two
  circles in a row, which the engine does not join.
- **force (§95):** f with a small s at the foot; the s turns back on itself and the plate
  does not show clearly enough whether it is the right or left s.
- **charge (§95):** one long straight downstroke, j-length: the same outline as the letter
  `j` (an existing example).
- **expect / especial (§95):** a small loop at the top of a p; whether an s comes between
  the circle and the p cannot be read.
- **look (§95):** l with a tiny reversed tick at its end; the end is too small to tell
  whether it is the oo hook alone or hook + k.
- **remember (§95):** r, small circle, long m: the same outline as the existing word
  `rim` (`r e m`).

## Changes to other levels

None. (`charge` and `remember` would duplicate the letter j and the word rim; they are
left out rather than merged across levels.)

## Is w written with the oo hook?

Not in the words of this unit: the hook stands only for ŭ, ŏŏ, ōō (§91), and none of the
§92/§94 words begins with w (who and whom are `h u`, `h u m`: the w is silent). The one
place w appears is the brief form **wish**, which shares `u sh` with usual: there the
initial w is the oo hook. (The stroke table already lists "initial w" as a sound of `u`.)

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- **s signs.** §49 gives no rule for s after the oo hook, so "all other cases" applies and
  the right s is used: does, plus, lose, dozen, us, thus, cousin. The plates agree (the s
  continues the hook's clockwise turn, §93's "without an angle"). In curse the s follows
  r: left s (§49 rule 1). s before the hook (supper, sullen) and before t (stuff) is the
  right s.
- **th.** thus is the right-motion th (`th-over`: no o, r, l beside it); through is
  `th-under` (before r, §78).
- **Circle sizes** follow the vowel group: canoe (ă) and annul (ă) large; sullen (ĕ) and
  clear small; care large.
- **full, sure** are read as f / sh + the oo hook with the l / r not written: both plates
  end in a small arch opening downward at the foot of the downstroke (like longhand h).
- **sugar** is `sh u g r`: the g and r follow the hook as in Unit 2's gr.
- **govern** is `g v`: g followed directly by v.

## Engine needs

The `u` hook (verify flag set) is drawn by default as the measured hook: rising ~30°, a
narrow turn over the top, coming back steeply down to the left (clockwise). What the
plates show about its joinings:

1. **§94, turned under after n and m** (none, noon, moon, nut, numb, annul, canoe, mood,
   muff, nook, null; p. 50). After n or m the hook is not raised above the line: it
   continues from the end of the n / m along the line and curls down and back under it,
   so the n / m runs into the hook without an angle, and the next stroke leaves from the
   bottom of the curl (none, numb look like a flattened "2" lying on the line). The rule is
   the counterpart of §67 for the o hook, but on the other side of the join: the hook is
   turned so that its **start** runs along the end of the n / m (the o hook was turned so
   that its end runs into the following stroke). Suggested rule: after n, m, men (and
   probably nd, md, the n/m blends — no plate), turn `u` so its start tangent matches the
   previous stroke's end tangent.
2. **§94, after k or g when followed by r or l** (cool, cur, curse; p. 50): the same turn
   under after k, but only when the next stroke is r or l. Not after k / g otherwise: cut,
   cook, cup, cousin (p. 49) show the ordinary raised hook after k.
3. **§93, us without an angle** (us, cousin, dozen, does, thus, plus, lose; pp. 48–49):
   the right s continues the hook's clockwise turn as one smooth curve ("ↄ"); suggested:
   add `u s-right` to the rounded pairs in join.js. The rule names the start of a word and
   after a downstroke or k, g; the plates after d, th and l (does, dozen, thus, lose, plus)
   look just as smooth.
4. **After downstrokes** (pull, foot, food, fur, fool, shut, sugar; p. 49): the hook sits
   at the foot of the p / f / sh as a small arch opening downward; the downstroke's end
   meets the rising side of the hook at a point (a sharp "λ"-like junction in fur, foot,
   shut), i.e. the ordinary hook with an angle — no turn is needed. full and sure (§95)
   show the same arch.
5. **Before downstrokes** (up, upper, cup, group, oven, rub, rough, tough, stuff, muff,
   enough): the hook's descending end runs straight on into the p / b / f / v with no
   visible angle; the default hook should already do this (its end heads down-left).
6. **Before forward strokes** (hug, whom, tomb, took, cook, luck, rug, truck; pp. 48–49):
   the hook comes down and the k / g / m leaves at an angle from its lower end; default
   geometry.
7. **h before the hook** (who, hug, whom): the dot above the hook, as with the o hook.

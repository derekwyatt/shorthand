# Manual Unit 13: the diphthongs (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter V, Unit 13, printed
pages 61–64 (PDF pages 77–80 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit2.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` circles, the
`o` / `u` hooks and the `h` dot, written in writing order; vowel marks are omitted. This
unit adds one token, `i`, for the broken circle (see Engine needs). Plate crop boxes
(80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in `scratchpad/u13/plates-unit13.json`, ids
`word-<word>` and `brief-<first word>`. Page 64 (§117 reading and dictation practice) is
connected plates only.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§112.** "A pure diphthong is the union in one syllable of two simple vowel sounds
  uttered in rapid succession. The diphthongs are therefore expressed by joining the
  circles and hooks representing the vowel sounds of which the diphthongs are composed":
  ū as in fume, ow as in now, oi as in oil, ī as in die.
  Note: "The diphthong u is a combination of ē and o͞o; ow, of ä and o͞o; oi, of aw and ē.
  The sign for the diphthong i is a large circle with an indentation—resembling a
  combination of ä and ē, which, if uttered in rapid succession, yield a sound almost
  equivalent to ī. This sign is generally called "the broken circle.""
  "The signs are written in their sounded order. The sign for the diphthong i is treated
  as a circle, and conforms to the rules for joining circles."
- **§113 Word drill.** Footnote to smile: "See mile in the brief forms below."
- **§114.** "For convenience, long i is expressed by the large circle in the following
  words": life, line, quite, might.
- **§115 Brief forms for common words.**
- **§116.** "When word forms end with the diphthong i, the double circle is used to
  express the diphthong and the termination ly": lightly, kindly, rightly, nightly.

**The diphthong signs in tokens.** ū = `e u` (small circle, then the oo hook); ow = `a u`
(large circle, then the oo hook); oi = `o e` (the o hook, then the small circle); ī = `i`.

## Left out, and why

- **prize (§113):** the plate carries the z dash, which has no token.
- **size, wise (§113):** the s signs are written tight against the broken circle and the
  §49 rule does not settle the motion of an s that touches only a circle; the plates at
  600 dpi do not show the motion clearly.
- **design (§113):** the d–e–s junction is too small to tell whether a circle is written
  before the s.
- **dining (§113):** the ing dot.
- **§116 lightly, kindly, rightly, nightly:** the double circle (a broken circle with a
  small circle inside it) needs a new token.
- **§117:** connected reading practice only.

## Engine needs

1. **The broken circle `i`.** A large circle (the `a` size) with a short inward
   indentation where the circle closes; per §112 it joins exactly like a circle (§11–18
   rules, the §71 reversal where it applies). It should be its own token so that
   assessment can tell it from `a` (§114 words use plain `a`).
2. **Circle then hook (`e u`, `a u`).** In fume, now, cow, mouth the hook is written
   straight on from the circle's closing point, opening up and to the right, so the pair
   reads like a small "σ" with a tail; after n, m the §94 turn-under must not apply to a
   hook that follows a circle.
3. **Hook then circle (`o e`).** In oil, boy, toy, join the small circle is written inside
   the curl at the end of the o hook ("ə" shape); after a downstroke (boy, voice, choice,
   enjoy) the hook sits at the foot of the stroke.
4. **The h dot** goes above the ū sign at the start of human and above the hook in white
   (as in Unit 11 wheel).

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- The plates of kind, night, why, find, behind, side and the other brief forms show the
  indentation and are read as `i`; life, line, quite, might (§114) show a plain large
  circle.
- smile follows the footnote: the l is dropped as in the brief form mile (`m i`), and the
  plate shows no l.
- human is read with the men blend (`h e u men`); the plate shows one long straight stroke
  after the ū sign.
- The s signs follow §49/§51: a final s after a circle belongs to the consonant before the
  circle (nice, price, rise, twice after t, realize after l: left s; noise, voice, choice
  after the o hook: left s). An initial s before a hook is the right s, as in Unit 10
  (so, sought) and Unit 11 (sweet).
- excited: ex is written es (§106), so `e s-right`, then `i` and the ted blend.
- inquire and require use the brief r k / n k joins as the plate shows (one stroke flowing
  into the circle); no vowel circle is written between them.
- Ambiguous readings to check first on the comparison sheet: human, soil, twice, excited,
  side, thousand (the first stroke is read as `th-over`), require.

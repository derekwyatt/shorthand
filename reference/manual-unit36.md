# Manual Unit 36: a short vocabulary (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter XII, Unit 36, printed
pages 155–161 (PDF pages 171–177 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit32.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` / `i`
circles, the `o` / `u` hooks, the `h` dot and the `w-dash`, written in writing order; vowel
marks are omitted. Plate crop boxes (80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in
`scratchpad/u36/plates-unit36.json`, ids `word-<slug of the title>`; they cover the Words
table only. The vocabulary runs over pp. 155–159, in alphabetical order, two columns a page;
p. 160 (§247) is reading and dictation practice and p. 161 (§248) writing practice.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§246 A short vocabulary.** "This short vocabulary will be a valuable addition to the
  equipment of every shorthand writer. Though many of these words are not of high
  frequency, it will be seen at a glance that they are of sufficient importance to warrant
  study. Many of them are written according to the abbreviating principle:" The list runs
  from abstract to wife, about 190 words (pp. 155–159).
- **§247 Reading and dictation practice**: connected text, not transcribed.
- **§248 Writing practice**: longhand text for the learner to write, not transcribed.

## Left out, and why

- **Same outline as an existing example:** coupon (`k p n`, = word-compound), premium
  (`p r e m`, = word-prim).
- **Two different words drawn alike on the plate**, so neither reading can be checked:
  crop and corporation (corporation is already `k o r p` in Unit 26), afraid and freight,
  distinct and distinguish, signature and significant.
- **Readings tried and dropped after comparing with the plate:** jury (`j u r` draws a long
  r the plate doesn't show), merchant (`m ch n`: the plate's final stroke rises, with a
  loop, where `n` lies level).
- **Readings tried and dropped, continued:** energy (the plate shows a hook at the top where
  `n e j` draws a circle), execute (the plate's last stroke is about three times as long as
  the k the reading gives).
- **h dot beside or before a stroke:** headquarters, husband, wholesale.
- **Capital mark above the outline, not under it:** English (the dashes sit over the

## Inference

- argument as `a g m`: the circle, the long g and the m; the r is dropped (§165) and the
  word ends with the m.
- clerk as `k l k`: a small k, the long l dipping below and a final small k; the vowel and
  the r are omitted (§165).
- commerce as `k m e-rev-s`: the k, the m and a narrow loop at the end of the m, read as the
  §74 s loop on a straight stroke.
- compare as `k p r`: com as k, the p, and the r turning forward at its foot.
- connect as `k n e k`: con as k, the n, the small circle and the k.
- defendant as `def nd`: the def blend and the nd blend running down from it.
- energy as `n e j`: a short level n, a slight dip for the vowel, and the long j.
- execute as `e s-right k`: ex as the small circle and s, then the k.
- inasmuch as `n s-right m ch`: the short n, the s turned back, the m and the ch down.
- legislate as `l j`: the l curving straight into the j with no circle visible.
- manufacture as `m f`: the long m and the f curving down from its end.
- occupy as `o k p`: the small o hook, the k and the p; occupation shares the outline.
- partial as `p a sh`: the p, the circle at its foot and the sh tick.
- vote as `v o t`: the v, a hook too small to see at its foot, and the t rising forward.

## Engine needs

None for the words above. Headquarters, husband and wholesale would need an h dot beside a
stroke; English the capital mark placed above the outline.

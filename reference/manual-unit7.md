# Manual Unit 7: the o hook (source transcription for level 12)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter III, Unit 7, printed
pages 34–38 (PDF pages 50–54 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit2.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` circles and
the `h` dot, in writing order; vowel marks (the dot under aw, the tick under ō) are
omitted, so words that differ only by a mark share one outline. `o` is the o hook. Plate
crop boxes (80 dpi, printed pages) are in `scratchpad/u7/plates-unit7.json`. Page 38 (§70)
is reading practice only.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§65.** "The lower half of the elliptical figure [ellipse] is called the o-hook. It is
  used to express the following sounds:" ŏ as in rot, aw as in raw, ō as in wrote. "Key to
  Vowel Sounds: John Paul Jones." Note: "The sound aw is spelled in various ways, as in
  fall, bought, taught, raw. The same method of marking vowels is employed in this chapter
  as in the first."
- **§66 Frequent-word drill.**
- **§67 O-Hook Modified.** "To avoid an unnecessary angle, the slant of the o-hook is
  modified slightly before n, m, r, and l, thus:" (four hooks lying on their side, opening
  to the right, running into n, m, r, l). "When a downstroke comes before the o-hook, this
  rule does not apply, since the o-hook joins to downstrokes without an angle, as in:
  pour, shown, bone, pole."
- **§68 Frequent-word drill.**
- **§69 Brief forms for common words.** Footnote: "The w is omitted in want and went."
- **§70 Reading and dictation practice** (p. 38): connected text only, not transcribed.

## Left out, and why

- **John, Paul, Jones (§65):** the key to the vowel marks; proper names.
- **box (§66):** x, which has no token (as in Unit 6).
- **sober (§66):** the s and the hook at the top of the b are tangled with the marks of
  motion above it; the s sign cannot be read.
- **§67 figure:** four modified hooks shown alone, not words.
- **Brief forms told, small, believe / belief, possible, purpose, receive, call,
  situation, course, general, several (§69):** told and call end in a short turn that
  cannot be told apart from a hook or a short r; small, possible, purpose, receive,
  situation, several have s signs or joinings too small to read with confidence; believe
  ends in a closed loop at the foot of the b that is not a joining the engine has; course
  ends in an unclear hook; general has a loop where j meets n.
- **§70 reading practice:** connected text.

## Changes to other levels

None. No earlier example used the o hook (it was unverified until now). The stroke table
still lists the hook alone as the brief form for of / all (Simplified chart); Unit 7 does
not mention it.

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- **S before the hook:** §49 names no rule for s before o, so "in all other cases the right
  s is used" applies; the plates of so, soul, solemn, solid start with a small turn to the
  right, which agrees. Store and story take the right s before t (§49 rule 2). After the
  hook, loss and close take the left s (§49: after o); notes, notice and horse take the
  left s after t and r; folks takes the right s after k.
- **Circle sizes:** every circle here is small (`e`: hotel, notice, coffee, sorry, story,
  solemn, solid, omitted, college, fellow, went) except the large initial circles of
  abroad and alone (`a`). The printed circles agree.
- **want, went (§69):** the w is omitted; want is read as the hook (the aw sound) plus the
  nt blend `nd`, went as `e nd`. The plates show an open hook and a closed small circle.
- **order** is the hook plus a long upstroke, read as `d`.

## Joins the engine may draw differently

- **Hook before nd (want):** the plate's hook is turned on its side, opening right, like
  the §67 hooks; the engine turns the hook only before n, m, r, l, so want starts with an
  upright hook.
- **Hooks between strokes are tiny at print size** (ball, abroad, bought, sorry): the plate
  hook after a downstroke is a small open curl; the engine's turned hook is narrower.
- **Crops:** the boxes for bought, brought, blow and ball take in the foot of the previous
  row's printed word, because the tall b reaches into that row.

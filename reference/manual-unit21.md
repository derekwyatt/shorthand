# Manual Unit 21: common prefixes and suffixes, and phrasing principles (source transcription)

Source: *Gregg Shorthand Manual, Anniversary Edition* (1929), Chapter VII, Unit 21, printed
pages 93–96 (PDF pages 109–112 of the scan at https://greggshorthand.github.io/Annivman.pdf).
A 1929 US publication, in the US public domain since 1 January 2025.

Conventions are as in `manual-unit2.md`: tokens are stroke ids plus `a` / `e` / `i`
circles, the `o` / `u` hooks and the `h` dot, written in writing order; vowel marks are
omitted. The w of we is the oo hook running into a circle (`u e`, the Unit 11 w loop).
Plate crop boxes (80 dpi, printed page numbers) are in `scratchpad/u21/plates-unit21.json`,
ids `word-<word>` and `phrase-<words>`. Pages 95–96 (§172–173) are reading and writing
practice.

## Rules, quoted from the manual

- **§170 Common prefixes and suffixes.** "The prefixes for, fore, fur are expressed by f.
  The suffixes ful and ify are expressed by f; self by s; selves by ses; and age by j:"
  Notes: "(1) The syllable ture is written tr. (2) The vowel in baggage is omitted to
  distinguish the form from package. (3) When for or fore is followed by a vowel, disjoin f
  close to the next character, as in forearm. When for or fore is followed by r or l, form
  an angle after f, as in forerunner, furlong."
- **§171 Phrasing principles.** "In phrases, the words ago, early, few, him, hope, sorry,
  want, sure, possible, are modified as shown below:"
- **§172 Reading and dictation practice**, **§173 Writing practice**: connected text, not
  transcribed.

## Left out, and why

- **Same outline as an existing example:** awful (`o f`, the same as `word-off`); yourself
  (`u s-right`, the same as Unit 10 us); you want (`u nd`, the same as the brief form
  wonder, Unit 14).
- **Plate too small or unclear to read:**
  - Words: forgive (the end after g is a lone small circle, no v visible), forgotten (the
    junction of g and the final upward curve), foresee and furniture (the f runs into a
    small loop that could be a circle or an s), helpful and thoughtful (the descender after
    l / t is one curve; p and f can't be separated), certify, simplify, myself (the end loop
    could be `i` or a circle with s), storage, manager, average.
  - Phrases: we hope (a dot over the w loop that no rule accounts for), I hope to hear,
    I am sorry, we are sorry, early reply, at an early date, days ago, weeks ago, months
    ago, years ago, day or two ago, week or two ago, as near as possible, few days, few
    months, few minutes: the modified ago / early / sorry / few / possible forms are too
    small to tell which strokes and circles they keep.
- **Plates run in one sweep:** notify, modify, dignify (on the plates each runs on in one
  sweep over the top; the reading with the o hook is uncertain and the engine's drawing
  doesn't match, so they wait).
- **§170 note 3** (forearm, forerunner, furlong): named, not printed.
- **§172–173:** connected text.

## Inference (not stated by the manual)

- The suffixes are written as the rule gives them: ful and ify `f`, self `s`, selves `ses`
  (the two s signs joined, as in the §52 ses blend: first by §49, second the other), age `j`.
- himself, herself, itself use the brief forms him (`h e m`), her (`h e r`), it (`t`) with
  the left s after m, r, t (§49). themselves: them is `th-over m`, then ses. ourselves:
  our is `r`. yourselves: your is `u`, and us after the hook is the right s (§93).
- wonderful and if you want / do you want use want = `nd` (as in §171 I want) and wonder =
  `u nd` (Unit 14).
- useful: use is the brief form `e u` (Unit 13).
- notify and modify: the small loop between n / m and the t / d is read as the o hook.
- to him, I told him, we told him: him is written `m` (§171); told shows only the t on the
  plate.
- be sure, we are sure, I am sure: sure is written `sh` without the hook.

## Engine needs

1. **ses after a stroke** (themselves, ourselves, yourselves): the §52 `s-left s-right` /
   `s-right s-left` rounded pairs should already draw it; check the ses sits at the end of
   m / r / the oo hook as on the plate.
2. **f after the o hook** (notify, modify): the t / d runs up and turns over into the f in
   one sweep on the plate; check the t f and d f joins aren't sharp.
3. **Least certain readings, to check first on the comparison sheet:** dignify, notify,
   modify, package, courage, I told him, if you want.
